\title{From Update Geometry to Retrieval Geometry:\\
Understanding Muon for Dense and Late-Interaction Text Embeddings}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{abstract}
Neural retrievers are usually adapted with AdamW, whereas matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon
promise faster and more robust optimization by changing the geometry of matrix updates. Whether
that inductive bias transfers to Adam-pretrained embedding models---and whether it affects dense and
late-interaction retrievers through the same mechanism---remains unclear. We conduct a controlled
\NumDiscoveryRuns-run study over two retriever architectures, three optimizers, four learning rates,
five training stages, and \NumBEIRTasks{} decontaminated zero-shot retrieval tasks. We then evaluate
every optimizer at common weight states and on common gradient histories, relate update spectra and
row balance to representation rank, margins, ranking drift, and MaxSim token utilization, and test
the resulting links with scale-matched interventions, shared-start branches, and a parameter-routing
control. \DiscoveryHeadline. \ConfirmationHeadline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AdamW is the default optimizer for training and adapting text embedding models. Its coordinate-wise
second-moment normalization is effective and familiar, but it ignores the matrix structure of
Transformer projections. Muon instead orthogonalizes momentum updates for hidden matrices, and
NorMuon augments that transform with neuron-wise adaptation
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. Large-scale language-model pretraining
suggests that these matrix-aware rules can improve compute efficiency. It does not follow that they
will improve retrieval fine-tuning: the starting model was pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer,
the contrastive objective depends on rankings rather than next-token likelihood, and dense and
late-interaction models expose different functional bottlenecks.

This paper therefore asks a mechanism question rather than staging another optimizer leaderboard:
\emph{how does matrix-aware optimization change the path from an Adam-pretrained encoder to a
retrieval function, and when are those changes useful?} We separate four spaces that are often
conflated: the update prescribed at the same parameters, the accumulated weight trajectory, the
resulting representation and score geometry, and the final retrieval ranking. This separation is
essential because large parameter-space distances can be functionally redundant, while small
directional changes can alter nearest-neighbor rankings.

Our study uses the unsupervised DenseOn and LateOn checkpoints
\citep{sourty2026denseon}, a shared deterministic \NumTrainingQueries-query training view with
\NumHardNegatives{} explicit negatives per query, and no in-batch negatives. The discovery stage
retains \NumDiscoveryCheckpoints{} checkpoints and evaluates all \NumDiscoveryUnits{}
checkpoint--task combinations. A second stage holds weights, batches, gradients, optimizer-state
history, and update budgets fixed to identify optimizer-specific update fingerprints. Finally,
functional interventions and short shared-start branches test whether these local fingerprints
survive into retrieval behavior.

Our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We provide a controlled dense/late-interaction benchmark of AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon,
    reporting learning dynamics, time-to-quality, memory, learning-rate tolerance, and complete
    decontaminated retrieval results.
    \item We distinguish Muon's singular-direction conditioning hypothesis from NorMuon's
    neuron-wise row-balancing hypothesis using common-state updates rather than distant checkpoint
    differences alone.
    \item We connect update geometry to retrieval-specific function geometry: dense-vector rank,
    hubness, and margins, plus token-level MaxSim evidence concentration for late interaction.
    \item We isolate update rule, parameter routing, update magnitude, and optimizer-pretraining
    mismatch through hybrid AdamW, scale-matched virtual steps, shared-checkpoint branches, and
    prospectively frozen multi-seed confirmation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Predictions}

\subsection{Three update geometries}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise preconditioner,
\begin{equation}
\Delta W_t^{\mathrm{AdamW}} =
-\eta\frac{\widehat{M}_t}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}_t}+\epsilon}-\eta\lambda W_t,
\end{equation}
whereas Muon applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum matrix,
approximately replacing its nonzero singular values by a common scale. NorMuon subsequently applies
row-wise second-moment normalization. Consequently, we predict that Muon increases the effective
rank and improves the conditioning of individual matrix updates relative to AdamW, while NorMuon
specifically reduces dispersion and concentration of row energy relative to Muon. A flatter update
spectrum is not assumed to be beneficial: it may amplify noise-dominated directions or disrupt
useful Adam-pretrained features \citep{qu2026finetune}.

\subsection{From update geometry to retrieval geometry}

The hypothesized chain is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{operator} &\rightarrow \text{update/trajectory} \\
                &\rightarrow \text{representation/score} \\
                &\rightarrow \text{ranking}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Dense retrieval compresses a sequence into one vector, so we measure embedding covariance rank,
isotropy, hubness, and query--positive margins. LateOn uses a ColBERT-style MaxSim interaction
\citep{khattab2020colbert}; we additionally measure how evenly query tokens contribute evidence and
how broadly document tokens are selected. A geometry metric is explanatory only when it predicts a
margin or ranking change, and checkpoint-level correlations remain observational.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Neural retrieval.}
Dense dual encoders score a query and document through compact single-vector representations,
whereas ColBERT-style retrievers preserve token vectors and aggregate token-wise maxima
\citep{khattab2020colbert}. BEIR established heterogeneous zero-shot evaluation as a central stress
test for retrievers \citep{thakur2021beir}; DenseOn and LateOn provide unusually controlled dense
and late-interaction starting points trained from an open recipe \citep{sourty2026denseon}.
Representation geometry has already been connected to zero-shot retrieval---for example, HIL
explicitly balances isotropic and anisotropic components \citep{kim2024hil}. We treat isotropy and
effective rank as explanatory variables rather than contributions by themselves, and require them
to connect optimizer interventions to scores or rankings.

\paragraph{Matrix-aware optimization.}
AdamW combines coordinate-wise moment adaptation with decoupled weight decay
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}. Muon instead exploits the matrix organization of hidden weights and
has shown favorable scaling in language-model pretraining \citep{liu2025muon}. NorMuon identifies
non-uniform row norms after orthogonalization and adds neuron-wise second-moment adaptation
\citep{li2025normuon}. Concurrent analyses of Adam-pretrained model adaptation report that a naive
optimizer switch can disrupt pretrained knowledge as update strength grows, and that
optimizer--model consistency can improve the learning--forgetting tradeoff
\citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. These works motivate our hypotheses, but none traces the complete path from
same-state matrix updates through dense and token-level retrieval rankings.

\section{Controlled Retrieval Study}

\subsection{Models, data, and objective}

DenseOn is a single-vector encoder and LateOn is a token-level late-interaction encoder. Both base
checkpoints have undergone unsupervised retrieval pretraining but not the supervised fine-tuning
studied here \citep{sourty2026denseon}. We select one deterministic view of
\NumTrainingQueries{} queries from the released training corpus. Every group contains one positive
and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct sampled negatives; all runs share the exact group identities and
order. We optimize an eight-way contrastive objective without in-batch negatives, use a maximum
context length of \ContextLength{}, a global batch size of 128, one epoch, bfloat16 forward passes,
FlashAttention, and gradient checkpointing.

\subsection{Discovery matrix}

For each architecture, we train AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon at four learning rates. AdamW uses
$\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$; Muon and NorMuon use
$\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$. Muon-family optimizers route hidden
two-dimensional Transformer matrices through the matrix rule and all auxiliary parameters through
AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. We retain checkpoints at 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of training.

All checkpoints are evaluated on the same \NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite
\citep{thakur2021beir}. We report nDCG@10 against steps, examples, useful wall time, and GPU-hours;
peak memory and optimizer-state size are reported separately. Full learning-rate trajectories are
shown, not only each optimizer's best point.

\subsection{Selection and confirmation}

The discovery grid is exploratory. We select one recipe per architecture and optimizer using a
frozen 4,096-query validation set that is disjoint from the 500K training ledger and does not use
BEIR scores. The selection rule minimizes eight-way validation loss, then maximizes positive margin,
with the smaller learning rate as the final tie-breaker. Three new seeds preserve query and positive
identities while independently resampling negatives from the reconstructed candidate pools. Only
these new-seed runs support the confirmatory optimizer comparison.

\section{What Do the Optimizers Change?}

\subsection{Integrated weight trajectories}

We stream all 120 checkpoints and analyze the 88 hidden matrices routed through Muon-family rules
separately from embeddings, norms, biases, and heads. For every tensor we measure displacement from
the pretrained state, row and column energy balance, concentration, and the coarse path joining the
pretrained state to the five retained checkpoints. Singular-spectrum fields are deliberately
disabled in this all-checkpoint tier; exact spectra are reserved for a frozen state--layer subset in
the common-state analysis.

Across \NumWeightPairs{} matched Muon/NorMuon checkpoint pairs, the NorMuon-to-Muon reference
displacement ratio lies in \DisplacementRatioRange. Despite this nearly identical aggregate scale,
the corresponding row-norm coefficient-of-variation ratio is \RowCVRatioRange, and the top-1\%-row
energy ratio is \TopRowEnergyRatioRange. The direction repeats at all five stages, four nominal
learning rates, and both architectures. This is evidence that NorMuon redistributes trajectory
energy across output rows without primarily changing total displacement; it is not yet evidence
that the redistribution causes a retrieval improvement.

\subsection{Common-state update fingerprints}

At the pretrained state and selected 20, 60, and 100\% checkpoints, we freeze weights and replay the
same ordered sequence of eight gradients into every optimizer. This frozen-weight state warm-up is
the formal common-state protocol; any one-gradient cold-start diagnostic is labeled auxiliary rather
than pooled with it. We keep weight decay separate and evaluate raw and per-tensor Frobenius-matched
directions. The primary measurements are effective rank, condition number, row-energy concentration,
layer allocation, and pairwise update angle. \CommonStateHeadline.

\section{Causal Controls}

\paragraph{Routing-matched AdamW.}
The main AdamW sweep applies one learning rate to every trainable parameter, whereas Muon-family
runs use an auxiliary AdamW route. An eight-run hybrid AdamW control adopts the identical partition
and auxiliary rate, isolating the matrix transform from parameter routing.

\paragraph{Scale-matched virtual steps.}
At each common-state anchor, we intervene along AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon directions while holding
weights, examples, gradients, and update norm fixed. Baseline, three positive scales, and a
sign-reversed condition are evaluated on the same 224 unseen queries. These one-step results identify
immediate directional effects but not long-horizon training behavior.

\paragraph{Shared-start branches.}
Both architectures branch from the same 60\% AdamW checkpoint. AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon receive
hidden learning rates calibrated to a common global $\lVert\Delta W\rVert_F/\lVert W\rVert_F$
target and train over the same 50K groups under three order seeds. All five checkpoints are scored
on the query-disjoint and unseen functional probes. \InterventionHeadline.

\section{Discussion}

The interpretation is selected by evidence rather than by optimizer preference. A Muon
time-to-quality gain with tied final retrieval would establish an optimization benefit but not a
better representation. Lower loss with worse BEIR performance would support optimizer mismatch and
functional drift. A NorMuon advantage concentrated in LateOn, accompanied by improved token-evidence
coverage, would support a retrieval-specific role for row adaptation. If update and weight geometry
change without consistent margin or ranking changes, the appropriate conclusion is that much of the
parameter-space difference is functionally redundant.

The practical recommendation will therefore condition on model family, update strength, and the
desired compute/quality operating point rather than naming a universal winner. \ConfirmationHeadline.

\section{Limitations}

The study uses two related 149M-parameter base models and one English supervised corpus, so it does
not establish behavior at substantially different scales, languages, or objectives. Both starting
models were pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer, making the conclusions directly relevant to
optimizer switching but not to Muon-from-scratch pretraining. Four learning rates provide a broad
but finite search, and three confirmation seeds may leave small effects uncertain. Intrinsic
geometry metrics are not causal by themselves; causal language is restricted to fixed-state
interventions and shared-start branches. Finally, decontamination reduces known overlap but cannot
prove the absence of every semantic duplicate.

\section{Ethics and Reproducibility}

The experiments adapt openly released retrieval models and datasets. We report all frozen task,
checkpoint, and learning-rate cells, including failures, and preserve model revisions, dataset
fingerprints, negative-sampling seeds, package versions, checkpoint hashes, and W\&B histories. The
confirmatory seed and recipe-selection protocol is kept separate from the exploratory BEIR matrix.
The released code supports strict artifact audits before producing paper tables or figures.
