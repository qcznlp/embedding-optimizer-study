\title{Beyond the First Step:\\
How Muon Changes Dense-Retriever Optimization Trajectories}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Muon reshapes matrix updates and can tolerate learning rates far above those used by AdamW, but its
effect on dense retrieval is not understood. We compare AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon while adapting
DenseOn on the same 500,000-query training view. The retrieval result is promising: the best Muon
and NorMuon runs improve mean decontaminated-BEIR nDCG@10 from 0.5899 to 0.5923 and 0.5934, winning
on 10 and 11 of 14 tasks, and three of four Muon-family rates reach the AdamW reference versus two
of four AdamW rates. The mechanism is not the obvious one. From identical weights and matched
update norms, AdamW produces the larger immediate mean margin gain, while repeated shared-start
Muon updates finish with a positive unseen-margin contrast in all three seeds. Muon-family models
also move roughly twice as far in weight space for nearly the same fixed-probe score drift. These
results point to an optimizer-induced trajectory effect rather than a superior isolated step.
However, familiar spectral signatures do not explain that effect: temporal prediction, controlled
spectral transplants, and a held-run retrieval bridge all fail their frozen tests. A separate
model-selection audit reveals why the useful Muon region is easy to miss: a packed eight-way
validator selects a damaging ten-times larger rate because its embeddings depend on batch
composition. Independent padding removes that high-dose advantage before candidate breadth grows.
We therefore use a fresh, independently padded 12-run matrix as the final adjudicator. The emerging
picture is that Muon's retrieval advantage, when it survives clean replication, comes from the
trajectory of states and gradients created by repeated updates---not from spectral flattening or a
better first step alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Muon works unusually well in our dense-retrieval sweep. At its best learning rate it improves mean
decontaminated-BEIR nDCG@10 over the best AdamW run on 10 of 14 tasks; NorMuon improves on 11. Across
the whole four-rate grid, both matrix-aware optimizers have a higher median endpoint and one more
rate reaches the AdamW reference. One Muon-family configuration reaches that reference after roughly
0.75 useful hours, compared with 1.41 hours for the fastest observed AdamW configuration. These are
the kind of results that make Muon interesting for embedding training.

The obvious explanation does not fit. AdamW treats coordinates independently, whereas Muon
approximately orthogonalizes momentum updates for hidden matrices
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon}. If that conditioning simply produced a better descent
direction, then at the same weights and update norm it should yield a larger immediate functional
gain. We observe the reverse: AdamW improves mean query margin more on a matched virtual step, even
though full Muon trajectories reach the better retriever. The scientific question is therefore not
only \emph{whether} Muon helps, but how a locally weaker direction becomes a globally better path.

Dense retrieval makes that question harder than ordinary loss minimization. Training observes one
positive document and a short list of labeled negatives, whereas deployment ranks against an entire
corpus. The relevant causal chain is
\begin{align*}
\text{update geometry} &\longrightarrow \text{shortlist scores}, \\
\text{shortlist scores} &\longrightarrow \text{selected checkpoint}, \\
\text{selected checkpoint} &\longrightarrow \text{corpus ranking}.
\end{align*}
Every arrow can fail, and repeated updates change the future states and gradients on which the next
update acts. We call this accumulated dependence an \emph{optimizer-induced trajectory effect}.
Our matched-step, shared-start, and weight/function-space results are consistent with such an effect:
Muon moves farther in parameters without proportionally larger score drift, changes which queries
occupy the adverse tail, and turns a weaker immediate mean step into a positive final margin contrast
in every shared-start seed. Conversely, the familiar story that spectral flattening itself mediates
the gain fails temporal, component-intervention, and held-run prediction tests.

Model selection introduces a second puzzle. A query-disjoint eight-way validator chooses the largest
tested Muon-family rate, not the retrieval-optimal region. Retraining those frozen recipes on three
independently resampled negative views produces a large, consistent loss to AdamW. At first this
resembles a cost of Muon's flexibility: it may be exceptionally good at suppressing seven observed
negatives while damaging the rest of the corpus.

That explanation is wrong for a more consequential reason. Before widening the candidate set, we
attempted to reproduce the original eight-way score with independently padded inputs. It could not
be reproduced. Under the historical flattened/packed path, placing the same text beside a different
example changes its cosine score by as much as 0.2119; with forced padding the discrepancy is
0.0020. The apparent high-rate validation advantage vanishes at width 7 on the padded path, and
adding candidates up to width 2,048 does not restore it. The historical study is reproducible for
its pinned implementation, but it cannot identify a clean optimizer effect.

The audit matters because it separates two claims that the original draft conflated. The good
full-corpus Muon region is a real observation under the historical training stack; the catastrophic
high-rate recipe is largely a selection-path artifact. To learn whether the positive retrieval result
itself survives without packed execution, we rerun the complete 12-configuration matrix with
independent padding in training, validation, checkpoint reload, and full-corpus evaluation. The
primary estimand averages all four predeclared rates per optimizer, with validation-selected recipes
secondary and five checkpoints exposing the trajectory.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item It identifies a promising DenseOn regime in which Muon and NorMuon outperform the best
    AdamW configuration across most decontaminated BEIR tasks and reach the AdamW reference earlier
    in their fastest observed runs.
    \item It shows that this advantage is trajectory-level rather than a better isolated descent
    step: same-state matched updates favor AdamW in immediate mean margin, while repeated shared-start
    Muon updates produce a positive final unseen-margin contrast in all three seeds.
    \item It rejects spectral flattening as the tested explanation and identifies a packed-validation
    failure that selects outside the useful Muon region, then subjects the positive result to a
    source-bound, independently padded 12-run replication.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why Dense Retrieval Makes Optimizer Claims Fragile}

\subsection{The loss sees a shortlist; evaluation sees a corpus}

For query $q_i$, positive $d_i^+$, and seven sampled negatives $C_i^-$, our contrastive objective is
Let $z_{id}=s(q_i,d)/\tau$. The loss is
\begin{align}
\ell_i &= -z_i^+ + \log Z_i, \\
Z_i &= \exp(z_i^+) + \sum_{d\in C_i^-}\exp(z_{id}).
\end{align}
Full-corpus retrieval instead depends on the rank of $d_i^+$ among all documents in
$\mathcal D_i$. Holding out queries prevents direct query memorization, but it does not make
$C_i^-$ representative of $\mathcal D_i\setminus C_i^-$. Consequently, validation loss can improve
while nDCG falls. We call a change in optimizer ordering between these measurements the
\emph{shortlist--corpus gap}.

There is a second interface: the score function must be the same in training, validation, and
retrieval. Modern encoders may take different computational paths for flattened/packed inputs and
independently padded examples. If batch composition changes a nominally per-text embedding, then
the validator is not merely a noisy proxy for corpus retrieval; it evaluates a different function.

\subsection{Update geometry is not an outcome}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise second-moment
preconditioner. Muon instead applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum
matrix. If $M=U\Sigma V^\top$, its idealized transform is $UV^\top$, which approximately equalizes
nonzero singular values \citep{jordan2024muon}. NorMuon adds a row-wise historical rescaling after
orthogonalization \citep{li2025normuon}.

These are operator properties, not retrieval explanations. A flatter update can amplify weak or
noisy directions, and large weight motion can leave the ranking nearly unchanged
\citep{pang2026htmuon,qu2026finetune}. We therefore distinguish four levels of evidence:
same-state update geometry, immediate score changes after a norm-matched virtual step, accumulated
trajectories from a shared checkpoint, and final corpus rankings. A mechanism must cross these
levels; recognizing Muon's spectrum is only an implementation check.

\section{Related Work}

Dense dual encoders compress queries and documents into single vectors and rank by similarity.
BEIR evaluates whether such representations transfer across heterogeneous retrieval tasks
\citep{thakur2021beir}. DenseOn provides the unsupervised, not-yet-fine-tuned checkpoint and open
training recipe used here \citep{sourty2026denseon}. Work on representation isotropy and effective
rank motivates possible bridges between optimization and retrieval, but those statistics are not
surrogates for ranking quality \citep{kim2024hil}.

Muon has shown attractive scaling in language-model pretraining, while NorMuon augments its
matrix-level transform with neuron-wise adaptation \citep{liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. Concurrent
work warns that switching an Adam-pretrained model to Muon can disrupt pretrained knowledge as
update strength grows \citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Other analyses relate Muon to
feature rank or margins, identify risks from spectral equalization, or argue for covariance-aware
and layer-dependent matrix geometry \citep{ruan2026features,pang2026htmuon,du2026newtonmuon,
massena2026schatten}. Our question is downstream and retrieval-specific: which geometric changes
survive optimizer state feedback, preserve unseen scores, and predict corpus ranking?

\section{A Staged Test Rather Than a Leaderboard}

\subsection{One controlled adaptation problem}

We adapt the released DenseOn unsupervised checkpoint on one deterministic view of
\NumTrainingQueries{} queries. Each group contains one positive and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct
sampled hard negatives. Queries are allocated proportionally across seven source datasets; a
candidate is eligible only when its mined score is below $0.95$ times the positive score, and the
seven negatives are sampled without replacement from the first ten eligible candidates using
stable seed-42 hashes. Every optimizer receives identical group identities and order. We use cosine
InfoNCE at temperature 0.02, no in-batch or cross-device negatives, maximum context length
\ContextLength{}, global batch size 128, one epoch, and bfloat16 forward passes.

The grid contains four learning rates per optimizer: $\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},
3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$ for AdamW and $\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$
for Muon and NorMuon. Muon-family runs route hidden two-dimensional matrices through the matrix
rule and auxiliary parameters through AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. Each run keeps checkpoints at
20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of 3,907 optimizer steps. Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same
\NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite.

\subsection{Four stages of evidence}

The historical discovery grid asks whether any learning-rate region is promising and records full
learning curves, systems costs, and failures. It is exploratory because BEIR is visible. A frozen
4,096-query validation set then selects one recipe per optimizer by minimum eight-way loss, maximum
margin, and finally smaller learning rate. Three new negative-sampling seeds test those recipes while
preserving query and positive identities.

The failed reproduction described below changes the role of this evidence. The packed historical
grid becomes a diagnostic dataset, not the primary optimizer comparison. The corrected grid repeats
all 12 rates with independent padding throughout. Its primary outcome is the all-rate optimizer
contrast with simultaneous max-$T$ uncertainty over tasks; independently padded validation
selection is secondary. This ordering was frozen before corrected outcomes existed.

Finally, the mechanism analysis asks a prediction question. Nine weight-space features are computed
for every corrected run and stage. Each is added to a baseline containing optimizer, stage, and
learning-rate dose, and evaluated by leave-dose-index-out prediction. A feature is supported only if
it lowers pooled held-out RMSE and improves at least three of four folds. Descriptive correlations
cannot pass this gate.

\section{Muon Reaches Better Retrievers---and Validation Misses Them}

\subsection{Direct retrieval tuning makes Muon look better}

\DiscoveryHeadline

\input{generated/discovery}

Across the four final checkpoints, median nDCG@10 is 0.5858 for AdamW, 0.5901 for Muon, and 0.5910
for NorMuon. At the best rate, Muon reaches 0.5923 and NorMuon 0.5934 versus AdamW's 0.5899;
the task-level counts are 10/4 and 11/3 wins/losses. The best-configuration five-stage average also
favors Muon and NorMuon (0.5910/0.5918 versus 0.5880), so the difference is not created by one lucky
final checkpoint. Three of four Muon-family rates reach the frozen AdamW reference, compared with
two of four AdamW rates.

The systems result is more nuanced. Muon and NorMuon achieve only 0.95$\times$ and 0.93$\times$
AdamW throughput on this stack, so they are not faster per training step. Their fastest observed
time to the AdamW reference is nevertheless about 0.75 hours versus 1.41 hours for AdamW because a
useful high-dose trajectory reaches the target by 40\%. We therefore distinguish computational
throughput from time-to-quality.

\subsection{A held-out selector chooses the losing regime}

The query-disjoint validator selects $3\!\times\!10^{-3}$ for both Muon variants, ten times the
retrieval-optimal rate. Across the four-rate sweep, lower validation loss orders AdamW rates in the
same direction as final BEIR (Spearman $-1.0$), but orders Muon and NorMuon rates in the opposite
direction ($+0.8$ for both). Margin exhibits the corresponding sign reversal.

The discrepancy survives three new training views when those frozen recipes are used. Muon loses
0.0306 nDCG@10 to AdamW (familywise 95\% CI $[-0.0464,-0.0138]$), and NorMuon loses 0.0304
($[-0.0446,-0.0138]$). These estimates are internally valid for the pinned historical recipe, but
their interpretation will change in the next section.

The reversal is not explained by simple training memorization or domain mismatch. Relative to each
Muon-family retrieval optimum, the high-rate model has worse training loss, better shortlist
validation loss, and worse BEIR. On five source-aligned tasks, all ten optimizer--source transitions
from $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ to $3\!\times\!10^{-3}$ suppress the selected hard negative and improve
the observed margin, while all ten reduce full-corpus nDCG. The mean margin change is $+0.0254$ and
the mean nDCG change is $-0.0255$. At this point the natural hypothesis is that seven negatives are
too narrow to select a high-dose matrix optimizer.

\section{Why Model Selection Hides the Useful Muon Regime}
\label{sec:execution-audit}

We designed a nested-candidate test of that hypothesis. For the same queries and checkpoints, it
expands the negative set from 7 to 10, 32, 128, 512, and 2,048 documents. The test had a necessary
first gate: independently padded width-7 scoring had to reproduce the historical eight-way result.
All 12 runs fail that gate, with a maximum metric discrepancy of 8.2864.

A minimal control isolates the cause. Scoring a text alone versus beside one other example changes
a packed-path cosine score by as much as 0.211914. With flattening disabled, the largest bfloat16
disagreement is 0.001953. More importantly, the scientific ordering changes before candidate width
changes: under padded width-7 scoring, the high-dose Muon model has \emph{higher} loss and lower
margin than its retrieval-optimal counterpart. Increasing the width to 2,048 does not produce the
predeclared joint reversal.

\input{generated/candidate-breadth}

This result eliminates the attractive missing-negative story for the selection failure. The packed
validator did not faithfully measure a small version of the corpus objective; it measured a
batch-dependent score function. Because the trainer used the same packed path, the historical
three-seed experiment remains evidence about that exact selection system, but its negative result is
not evidence that Muon lacks a useful retrieval regime. Query disjointness and more random seeds
cannot repair a mismatch in the function being selected.

The only defensible adjudication is a new experiment. We therefore repeat every optimizer--rate cell
in a fresh namespace with flattening disabled during training and every checkpoint reload. The data,
schedule, hardware allocation, negative identities, and evaluation suite remain fixed. The primary
comparison averages all four predeclared rates; a clean, independently padded validator supplies a
secondary recipe comparison. Five-stage results distinguish a stable optimizer effect from a
single-checkpoint accident.

\section{A Trajectory Effect, but Not a Spectral Explanation}

\subsection{Large parameter motion need not be large functional motion}

At the historical retrieval-optimal points, mean absolute score drift on a fixed unseen probe is
0.0604 for AdamW, 0.0619 for Muon, and 0.0606 for NorMuon. Hidden-weight displacement from the
pretrained model is 0.00960, 0.01881, and 0.01885, respectively. Muon-family models therefore move
roughly twice as far in weight space for nearly the same change on this probe. At the largest rate,
weight distance continues rising while score drift falls and BEIR partly recovers. Distance is an
optimization-dose measurement, not an explanation of retrieval.

\subsection{Same-state steps reject the simplest mechanism}

At ten pretrained or trajectory anchors, we replay the same ordered gradient history into all three
optimizers, remove weight decay, and match update norms. Muon and NorMuon display distinct spectra
and row-energy allocation, but their mean immediate margin gains are 0.0006 and 0.0005 versus
AdamW's 0.0009. Their adverse query sets differ rather than uniformly improving the same tail.
Thus the historical retrieval difference cannot be explained by a better average next step from the
same state.

\subsection{Accumulation helps some tails, but spectra do not carry the explanation}

Shared-start branches begin at the same 60\% AdamW checkpoint, match the initial global relative
update budget, and train on the same 50,000 groups under three order seeds. Muon improves the final
unseen-margin contrast in all three seeds, but the predeclared joint tail endpoint is mixed; NorMuon
is mixed as well. A routing-matched hybrid AdamW changes mean nDCG by only $+0.000077$, ruling out
the hidden-matrix/auxiliary-parameter partition as a sufficient recipe explanation in this grid.

We then test the more specific claim that Muon's spectral redistribution causes the accumulated
effect. Early tail singular-value energy fails to improve leave-one-seed-out prediction of final
loss p95 or unseen margin p05. At fixed weights, interpolating Muon singular values into the AdamW
basis produces neither the required dose response nor tail-band localization, and spectrum features
do not improve held-run prediction of the next BEIR checkpoint beyond the basis controls. The
predeclared chain is therefore a claimable negative: spectral flattening is visible, but it does not
explain retrieval in these interventions.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Muon's advantage is real evidence, but not yet a universal ranking.}
The historical sweep shows a coherent useful region: better best-run and median retrieval, more
rates reaching the AdamW reference, favorable five-stage averages, and gains on most BEIR tasks.
That is stronger than one cherry-picked endpoint. It is still exploratory because the evaluation
suite reveals the best rate and because training used the packed path. The independently padded
matrix is therefore the primary comparison that decides how far the positive result generalizes.

\paragraph{A larger usable learning-rate range changes model-selection risk.}
Muon's ability to train at a large numerical learning rate is not equivalent to validation
robustness. A wider effective dose range can reach quality earlier, but also gives a biased or
execution-mismatched proxy more room to select a damaging endpoint. Retrieval studies should report
the full optimizer--rate surface and trajectory, not only the best cell, and should separate an
all-rate comparison from selector-dependent recipes.

\paragraph{Batch invariance is part of the estimand.}
For a dual encoder, users reasonably assume that a document embedding is a function of that document
and the model. Packed execution violated that assumption strongly enough to alter optimizer
selection. A two-example batch-composition test should therefore be a preflight check whenever
training, validation, and indexing use different collation paths.

\paragraph{Geometry must earn explanatory status.}
Muon's spectrum and NorMuon's row adaptation describe how their updates are constructed. Our
negative bridges show why reproducing these signatures during embedding training is not a research
contribution by itself. The useful question is whether geometry predicts functional tails and
corpus ranking outside the rates and stages on which it was measured. If no feature passes that
test, the correct conclusion is that the optimizer effect remains unexplained, not that the most
visually distinctive statistic must be the cause.

\section{Conclusion}

Muon and NorMuon reach better DenseOn retrievers than AdamW across a coherent part of the historical
learning-rate surface, including gains on 10 and 11 of 14 tasks. Their advantage does not come from
a better norm-matched first step: AdamW has the larger immediate mean margin gain, whereas repeated
Muon updates alter the state and gradient sequence and finish ahead on the shared-start unseen-margin
endpoint. The obvious geometric explanation also fails---larger displacement and flatter spectra
identify the optimizer but do not predict the retrieval gain in our frozen interventions. Finally,
a batch-dependent packed validator selects far outside Muon's useful regime, showing why optimizer
quality and optimizer selection must be evaluated separately. The corrected independently padded
matrix is the sole basis for the final recommendation, but the central mechanistic lesson is already
sharper than an optimizer leaderboard: Muon's promise lies in the trajectory created by repeated
updates, not in spectral flattening or a superior isolated step alone.

\section{Limitations}

Primary inference concerns one 149M-parameter DenseOn checkpoint, one English supervised corpus,
and one epoch of full-parameter adaptation. The base model was pretrained with an Adam-family
optimizer, so this is a study of optimizer switching rather than Muon-from-scratch pretraining.
Four learning rates provide a broad but finite grid. The corrected all-rate contrast reduces
selector dependence but remains a one-seed, pinned-grid result; the secondary selected-recipe
comparison does not replace replication across training views.

Decontamination reduces known benchmark overlap but cannot establish the absence of every semantic
duplicate. Aggregate MTEB outputs lack per-query rankings, so uncertainty is computed over tasks and
we make no query-level BEIR significance claim. The candidate-breadth study was designed after the
shortlist--corpus gap was observed and uses mined documents with incomplete relevance judgments. It
is possible for an apparent negative to be an unjudged relevant document. The analysis is a
falsification diagnostic, not a confirmatory estimate of candidate-set size.

The historical packed and corrected padded studies are not randomized assignments of execution
mode: they were run sequentially after the reproduction failure. Their difference is sensitivity
evidence, not a causal effect of packing. Historical shared-start and spectral analyses use nine
branches, three order seeds, and fixed-state interventions. They can reject the tested mechanism but
cannot identify formal mediation of a final BEIR contrast. The corrected geometry bridge is
predictive rather than causal and may miss a useful feature outside the nine frozen candidates.

The project originally included LateOn. After exploratory outputs were visible, the project scope
was narrowed to DenseOn because LateOn was substantially slower and less central to the intended
paper. This user-directed, post-hoc amendment is not a preregistered scientific exclusion. LateOn is
not pooled with DenseOn or used as a replication; its configurations and artifacts remain available
for audit.

\section{Ethical Considerations}

This study does not collect new human-subject data or deploy a retrieval system to users. Released
models and corpora can nevertheless contain social biases, harmful material, copyrighted text, or
personal information. Higher benchmark nDCG is not evidence of fairness, safety, privacy, or
deployment readiness. A production retriever requires corpus-specific auditing, filtering, access
controls, and human review appropriate to its users and failure costs.

We report useful wall time, throughput, GPU-hours, and optimizer-state size because an optimizer
recommendation has resource consequences. These measurements are specific to the pinned hardware
and software stack and do not justify unbounded hyperparameter search. Public artifacts must respect
the licenses and usage terms of upstream models, datasets, and evaluation collections.
