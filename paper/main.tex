\title{A Packed Validation Trap in Dense Retriever Adaptation:\\
Auditing AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Dense retrievers learn from a small shortlist of labeled negatives but are judged against an entire
corpus. We ask whether changing the optimizer changes calibration between these regimes, comparing
AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon while adapting DenseOn on one deterministic
\NumTrainingQueries-query view. Direct full-corpus selection gives modest exploratory Muon-family
gains, whereas query-disjoint eight-way validation selects a ten-times larger learning rate whose
retrieval is worse. A frozen nested-candidate follow-up fails its prerequisite: independently padded
width-7 scoring cannot reproduce the historical flattened/packed validation path. A two-example
control changes a cosine score by up to 0.2119 under packed execution versus 0.0020 when padding is
forced. On the padded path the apparent high-dose advantage is already absent at width 7, and
widening to 2,048 candidates does not reverse it. Thus candidate coverage does not explain the
gap; the historical comparison is evidence about the exact pinned implementation, not yet a clean
eight-way optimizer effect. Weight/function-space and frozen intervention analyses further reject
the tested spectral-dose account. \DiscoveryHeadline \ConfirmationHeadline The project originally
included exploratory LateOn runs; a user-directed, post-hoc scope amendment excludes them from
primary and confirmatory inference.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AdamW is the default optimizer for adapting text embedding models. Muon instead approximately
orthogonalizes momentum updates for hidden matrices, and NorMuon follows that transform with
neuron-wise adaptation \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. Their matrix-aware
updates have shown attractive scaling and memory behavior in language-model training. Dense
retrieval introduces a different constraint: the learner sees one positive and a small set of hard
negatives, whereas deployment ranks that query against millions of documents. A lower contrastive
loss is useful only when its ordering transfers beyond the observed shortlist.

Recent work shows that switching an Adam-pretrained model to Muon can disrupt pretrained knowledge
as update strength grows \citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. That observation does not answer
the retrieval question. A retriever may preserve its score function on a fixed eight-document probe
yet change the ordering of unseen corpus documents, or it may move far in weight space while making
a small functional change. We therefore treat optimizer geometry, shortlist metrics, and
full-corpus rankings as separate objects rather than interchangeable measures of progress.

Our historical result appears to be an optimizer-specific calibration failure. On the
query-disjoint validation shortlist, minimizing loss selects learning rate
$3\!\times\!10^{-3}$ for both Muon and NorMuon; direct full-corpus discovery instead selects
$3\!\times\!10^{-4}$. Moving between these rates suppresses the selected hard negatives and
improves the observed margin in every inspected optimizer--source transition, but lowers full-corpus
nDCG in every transition. A later frozen candidate-breadth test, however, exposes an upstream
implementation confound: the packed validation path is not batch invariant and cannot be reproduced
by independent padded scoring of the same texts. The three-seed comparison therefore estimates the
consequence of recipes selected by that historical path. It does not establish that Muon
intrinsically makes a clean eight-way objective anti-calibrated.

The remaining geometry is not a restatement of Muon's spectral equalization. At the
retrieval-optimal points,
all three optimizers induce almost the same score drift on a fixed unseen probe, while Muon and
NorMuon move about twice as far from the pretrained weights. At excessive learning rates, weight
distance continues to rise even as score drift falls and retrieval partly recovers, so parameter
distance alone is not an explanation either. This motivated an optimization-dose account, but the
frozen temporal and intervention chain is negative. The implementation audit instead identifies a
concrete failure mode that must be removed before optimizer-specific generalization mechanisms can
be inferred.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A controlled DenseOn comparison of AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon over a complete learning-rate
    sweep, five-stage training dynamics, and a decontaminated BEIR evaluation, with optimizer-state,
    throughput, and time-to-quality reported separately.
    \item A source-bound failure analysis showing that the apparent shortlist--corpus gap is
    confounded by batch-dependent flattened/packed execution; corrected padded width-7 scoring
    removes the high-dose advantage, while widening to 2,048 candidates does not support the
    missing-candidate account.
    \item A weight/function-space decomposition showing that useful Muon-family solutions reach a
    similar fixed-probe score drift through substantially larger parameter displacement, while
    displacement alone fails to predict high-dose retrieval behavior.
    \item Falsifiable tests of the optimization-dose account using shared-start scale-matched
    branches, query-tail outcomes, spectral dose/band/basis transplants, and a prospectively declared
    held-run bridge. Failed links remain reportable negative results rather than being replaced
    post hoc.
    \item Routing-matched hybrid AdamW that measures grouping and auxiliary-rate effects within
    AdamW and supplies a separately tuned, matched-routing comparator for Muon-family recipes,
    together with three new negative-sampling seeds that keep the final optimizer comparison
    independent of BEIR-based discovery selection.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Mechanistic Predictions}

\subsection{Three update rules}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise preconditioner,
\begin{equation}
\Delta W_t^{\mathrm{AdamW}} =
-\eta\frac{\widehat{M}_t}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}_t}+\epsilon}-\eta\lambda W_t.
\end{equation}
Muon instead applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum matrix. If
$M=U\Sigma V^\top$, the idealized transform is $\operatorname{polar}(M)=UV^\top$, approximately
equalizing nonzero singular values \citep{jordan2024muon}. The practical bfloat16 polynomial is an
approximation rather than an exact projection. NorMuon then forms a row-wise historical second
moment and rescales the orthogonalized update before restoring a matrix-level scale
\citep{li2025normuon}.

Spectral equalization is intrinsic to Muon's operator, and row-wise historical adaptation is
intrinsic to NorMuon. Neither fact establishes better retrieval. A flatter update can amplify weak
or noisy directions, and row balancing can change strongly without affecting a ranking
\citep{pang2026htmuon,qu2026finetune}. We accordingly treat spectra and row statistics as operator
fingerprints. They become explanatory only if a controlled change reaches margins or rankings and
persists under repeated updates.

\subsection{Shortlist loss versus corpus retrieval}

For query $q_i$, positive document $d_i^+$, and sampled negative set $C_i^-$, the training and
validation objective observes only
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \ell_i &=-s(q_i,d_i^+)/\tau+\log Z_i,\\
  Z_i &=\exp(s(q_i,d_i^+)/\tau)
       +\sum_{d\in C_i^-}\exp(s(q_i,d)/\tau).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Full-corpus retrieval instead depends on the rank of $d_i^+$ among every document in a corpus
$\mathcal D_i$. Holding out queries prevents direct query memorization, but it does not ensure that
$C_i^-$ represents the score changes over $\mathcal D_i\setminus C_i^-$. Consequently, two
optimizers can agree on the observed positive and selected hard negatives while changing the
unobserved score field differently.

We call the difference between optimizer ordering under $\ell_i$ and under full-corpus nDCG the
\emph{shortlist--corpus gap}. It is a selection failure, not a claim that the shortlist loss is
incorrect: the loss is exactly the objective supplied to the optimizer. A candidate-breadth
diagnostic expands a fixed query's nested comparison set from the original seven sampled negatives
to the ten-candidate source pool and then to 32, 128, 512, and 2,048 candidates from the same pinned
mined list. If missing-candidate coverage drives the reversal, the apparent high-dose advantage
should shrink or reverse as breadth grows. Because this diagnostic was designed after the initial
gap was observed, we label it post hoc, keep it outside confirmatory inference, and exclude its
descriptive source-stratified 95\% intervals (50,000 resamples) from the decision rule.

\subsection{From directions to trajectories}

We compare optimizer directions at identical weights and matched Frobenius budgets, then distinguish
their mean and adverse-tail query effects. These one-step interventions identify immediate direction
differences but not accumulated adaptation, because every committed update changes future gradients
and optimizer state. Shared-start branches test accumulation; spectral dose, band, and basis
transplants test whether a specific update component reaches the retrieval function. Geometry is
explanatory only when it predicts held-out outcomes beyond optimizer labels and norm controls.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Dense retrieval.}
Dense dual encoders compress queries and documents into single vectors and rank by their similarity.
BEIR established heterogeneous zero-shot retrieval as a demanding test of whether adaptation
preserves broadly useful ranking behavior \citep{thakur2021beir}. DenseOn provides an unsupervised,
not-yet-fine-tuned starting checkpoint with an open training recipe
\citep{sourty2026denseon}. Representation geometry has been linked to retrieval quality, including
work balancing isotropic and anisotropic components \citep{kim2024hil}; here, rank and isotropy are
possible bridge variables rather than endpoints.

\paragraph{Matrix-aware optimization and optimizer switching.}
Muon exploits hidden-matrix structure and has shown favorable scaling in language-model pretraining
\citep{liu2025muon}; NorMuon adds neuron-wise adaptation after orthogonalization
\citep{li2025normuon}. Concurrent studies warn that switching an Adam-pretrained model to Muon can
degrade downstream performance as update strength increases
\citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Other analyses connect Muon to larger margins and feature
rank \citep{ruan2026features}, or warn that spectral equalization can overemphasize noise-dominated
directions \citep{pang2026htmuon}. Newton--Muon highlights missing input-covariance preconditioning
\citep{du2026newtonmuon}, while adaptive Schatten geometry argues against a single matrix rule for
every layer \citep{massena2026schatten}. Our contribution is not another intrinsic description of
these operators; it is the controlled path from a same-state direction, through query-level effects
and accumulated adaptation, to dense retrieval rankings.

\section{Controlled Dense Retrieval Study}

\subsection{Scope amendment}

The project was originally designed as a two-architecture DenseOn/LateOn study. After the original
discovery runs and some exploratory mechanism outputs were visible, the user directing the project
requested that subsequent work focus on dense retrieval because LateOn training was substantially
slower and the intended paper audience placed less value on that architecture. This is a
\emph{user-directed, post-hoc scope amendment}, not a preregistered scientific exclusion. We preserve
the LateOn configurations, checkpoints, logs, and exploratory tables for audit, but do not pool them
with DenseOn, use them as replication, estimate architecture interactions from them, or let them
affect primary/confirmatory wording. All main-text hypotheses, accumulated branches, routing controls,
and three-seed inference below concern DenseOn only.

\subsection{Model, data, and objective}

DenseOn has undergone unsupervised retrieval pretraining but not the supervised adaptation studied
here \citep{sourty2026denseon}. We select one deterministic view of \NumTrainingQueries{} queries
from the released corpus. Each group contains one positive and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct sampled
negatives. We allocate queries proportionally over the seven source datasets, reject a candidate
unless its mined score is below $0.95$ times the positive score, and sample without replacement from
the first ten eligible candidates using stable seed-42 hashes. All discovery runs share group
identities and order. DenseOn uses cosine InfoNCE at temperature 0.02, without in-batch or
cross-device negatives, a maximum query/document context length of \ContextLength{}, global batch
size 128, one epoch, bfloat16 forward passes, FlashAttention, and gradient checkpointing.
The historical trainer and query-disjoint validator pass tokenized groups through
SentenceTransformers with input flattening enabled. Full-corpus retrieval and the later
candidate-breadth evaluator use independently padded examples. This distinction was not part of
the original optimizer hypothesis; Section~\ref{sec:execution-audit} reports the post-failure audit
that revealed its consequence.

\subsection{Discovery matrix and evaluation}

We train AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon at four learning rates each. AdamW uses
$\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$; Muon and NorMuon use
$\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$. Muon-family optimizers route hidden
two-dimensional Transformer matrices through the matrix rule and auxiliary parameters through
AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. We retain checkpoints at 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of training. Runs
use four GPUs, micro-batch 8 per GPU, four accumulation steps, 10\% linear warmup, linear decay, and
global gradient clipping at 1.0. The final partial accumulation is retained, so all 500K groups are
consumed in 3,907 optimizer steps.

Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same \NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite
\citep{thakur2021beir}. We report nDCG@10 against steps, examples, useful wall time, and GPU-hours,
and report peak memory and optimizer-state size separately. Full learning-rate trajectories are
retained; best-test-cell selection is never treated as confirmatory evidence.

\subsection{Selection and three-seed confirmation}

The discovery grid is exploratory. One recipe per optimizer is selected using a frozen 4,096-query
validation set disjoint from the 500K training ledger and without access to BEIR scores. The rule
first minimizes eight-way validation loss, then maximizes positive margin, with the smaller learning
rate as the final tie-breaker. Three new seeds preserve query and positive identities while
independently resampling negatives from reconstructed candidate pools. Only these new-seed DenseOn
runs support the confirmatory AdamW--Muon and AdamW--NorMuon comparisons.

We independently resample seeds and tasks in a deterministic 20,000-draw hierarchical bootstrap and
report nominal and familywise 95\% intervals for the two primary optimizer contrasts. The Bonferroni
interval retains all six comparisons fixed before the post-hoc Dense-only scope amendment, even
though only DenseOn rows enter the paper. Positive, negative, or inconclusive headline wording is
determined only after the frozen three-seed matrix is complete. Aggregate MTEB outputs do not contain
per-query rankings, so we make no query-level significance claim for BEIR.

\subsection{Frozen causal-chain protocol}

Nine shared-start branches provide 45 adjacent displacements. The frozen temporal test asks whether
early hidden-matrix tail spectral energy predicts final query-disjoint loss p95 and unseen margin p05
under leave-one-seed-out evaluation, beyond optimizer labels and update/weight norm controls. A
separate fixed-state test interpolates Muon singular values into the AdamW basis and requires
prespecified dose monotonicity, tail-band localization, spectrum-over-basis specificity, and improved
leave-one-source-run-out prediction of next-checkpoint BEIR. Every condition and negative control is
reported; failure of any required link rejects the spectral-component account rather than triggering
a replacement explanation. Full decision rules appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:causal-chain-diagnostics}.

\subsection{When the validation proxy wins and retrieval loses}

The discrepancy is systematic across the completed discovery sweep. Spearman correlation between
validation loss and final BEIR is $-1.0$ for AdamW, so lower loss orders its four learning rates in
the desired retrieval direction. The correlation is $+0.8$ for both Muon and NorMuon. Validation
margin shows the corresponding sign reversal: $+1.0$ for AdamW and $-0.8$ for each Muon-family
optimizer. Thus the same query-disjoint proxy is calibrated for one update rule and anti-calibrated
for the other two over this grid.

Post-hoc high-dose-minus-oracle training-loss/validation-loss/BEIR deltas are
$+0.1210/-0.3696/-0.0315$ for Muon and $+0.1068/-0.4268/-0.0300$ for NorMuon. Thus sampled-tuple
fit worsens while shortlist validation improves and corpus retrieval worsens, ruling out simple
memorization as a sufficient account but, because all inputs were already visible, not identifying
the cause.

We also isolate five source-aligned tasks for which the validation queries and full retrieval corpus
come from the same underlying source. Comparing the validation-selected $3\!\times\!10^{-3}$ rate
with the retrieval-optimal $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ rate yields ten optimizer--source transitions. In
all ten, the selected hardest negative is suppressed and the eight-way positive margin increases;
in all ten, full-corpus nDCG decreases. The mean selected-negative score change is $-0.0369$, the
mean positive-score change is $-0.0115$, the mean margin change is $+0.0254$, and the mean nDCG
change is $-0.0255$. Because positive scores increase in three transitions, the effect is not merely
uniform positive forgetting. These are exploratory, post-selection diagnostics, but they rule out
ordinary source-domain shift as a sufficient explanation of the observed gap.

\section{From Same-State Updates to Query Effects}

\subsection{Weight distance is not functional distance}

At each optimizer's retrieval-optimal discovery point, the mean absolute score drift on the fixed
224-query unseen probe is 0.0604 for AdamW, 0.0619 for Muon, and 0.0606 for NorMuon: a relative
range of only 2.5\%. The corresponding pretrained-reference hidden-weight displacement ratios are
0.00960, 0.01881, and 0.01885. Across all five checkpoints of these runs, the median ratio of
weight displacement to score drift is 1.90 times AdamW for Muon and 1.94 times AdamW for NorMuon.
The matrix-aware optimizers therefore use substantially more parameter motion for a similar change
on this fixed functional probe.

This relationship is not monotone outside the useful regime. At $3\!\times\!10^{-3}$, Muon-family
weight distance keeps increasing after the first checkpoint while fixed-probe score drift falls and
BEIR partly recovers. We therefore use displacement as an optimization-dose diagnostic, not as a
surrogate outcome. The shortlist--corpus hypothesis specifically predicts that a fixed eight-way
probe can miss score changes over documents that never enter its candidate set.

\subsection{Same-state directions and query tails}

At ten pretrained or trajectory anchors, we freeze weights and replay the same eight-gradient
history into every optimizer, separating weight decay and matching direction norms. Muon-family
directions have much flatter spectra but do not exceed AdamW's mean immediate margin gain. Symmetric
worst-tail comparisons instead show redistribution: each optimizer helps some queries that are
fragile under the other while selecting a different adverse set. A post-hoc cosine analysis is
consistent with optimizer-induced state feedback, but neither spectrum nor direction angle is a
retrieval explanation by itself. \CommonStateHeadline

\subsection{Routing-matched AdamW}

Native AdamW applies one learning rate to every trainable parameter, whereas Muon-family runs route
hidden matrices through one rule and auxiliary parameters through AdamW. A hybrid AdamW control uses
the identical partition and auxiliary learning rate. Native AdamW versus hybrid AdamW therefore
measures routing and auxiliary-rate effects within AdamW at each shared AdamW learning rate. Hybrid
AdamW versus Muon matches routing and the auxiliary rate, but each rule retains its independently
swept learning-rate range; it compares separately tuned recipes rather than identifying
orthogonalization, the matrix rule, or update scale alone. A full-length scale-matched component
control would be needed for that attribution. The present control removes a routing confound from
the recipe comparison but is not by itself a causal decomposition of Muon. Across the four shared
AdamW learning rates, hybrid-minus-native mean nDCG@10 is $+0.000201$, $-0.000331$, $+0.000383$, and
$+0.000055$. The aggregate change over all 56 task--learning-rate units is $+0.000077$ (28 wins, 6
ties, and 22 losses), and the best AdamW point changes only from 0.589867 to 0.589922. Thus routing
and the fixed auxiliary rate are far too small and inconsistent to account for the optimizer-level
retrieval differences.

\subsection{Representation and score geometry}

On fixed in-distribution and unseen probes, we measure the full query-margin distribution, embedding
effective rank and isotropy, hubness, and top-$k$ agreement with the pretrained model. We join these
quantities to DenseOn trajectories and separately analyze within-run first differences.
The complete numerical headline and representation readout are reported in
Appendix~\ref{tab:representation-results}.

Checkpoint correlations are observational. A representation metric enters the mechanism only if it
changes under a controlled direction or shared-start branch and aligns temporally with a later
functional effect.

\section{Discussion}

Discovery suggests a small Muon-family advantage under direct full-corpus tuning, whereas the
historical validator selects a ten-times larger rate with worse retrieval. The three-seed experiment
estimates the consequence of that frozen recipe, but the execution audit changes its scope: the
validator and trainer used a batch-dependent packed path, so the comparison is reproducible for the
pinned stack but cannot establish an intrinsic failure of a clean eight-way objective.

Same-source transitions and a post-hoc corpus-size association ($\rho=.587/.574$;
Appendix~\ref{fig:corpus-size-diagnostic}) do not prove missing-candidate coverage. The frozen nested
test rejects that account: its width-7 bridge fails and padded scoring removes the apparent high-dose
advantage before any candidates are added. Candidate breadth remains a design variable, but it is
not the explanation supported by these data.
\CandidateBreadthDiscussion

At useful points, Muon and NorMuon move roughly twice as far for nearly the same fixed-probe score
drift, yet high-dose trajectories decouple distance and drift. The failed shared-start and spectral
chain therefore rejects that mechanism, not the observed calibration failure.

Lower training-loss variation does not imply retrieval robustness. In practice, dense fine-tuning
should first disable or validate packed execution with batch-composition invariance controls, then
use retrieval-aware validation. Broader candidates may still help training, but do not repair this
historical gap; a larger usable learning-rate range does not make its validation-selected maximum
safe.

\section{Limitations}

Primary inference concerns one 149M-parameter DenseOn checkpoint, one English supervised corpus,
and one epoch of full-parameter adaptation. It does not establish behavior at other scales,
languages, objectives, pooling schemes, or sparse and late-interaction retrievers. The base model was
pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer, so the study tests optimizer switching rather than
Muon-from-scratch pretraining. Four learning rates provide a broad but finite search, and three
confirmation seeds may leave small effects uncertain. Intrinsic geometry metrics are not causal by
themselves; causal language is restricted to fixed-state interventions and shared-start branches.
Decontamination reduces known overlap but cannot prove the absence of every semantic duplicate.
The nested candidate-breadth diagnostic was designed after observing the shortlist--corpus gap and
therefore cannot confirm the optimizer comparison or establish mediation. Its mined lists designate
one positive per query but do not exhaustively judge every other document; an apparent negative can
contain an unjudged relevant passage. The experiment tests score-field coverage under a fixed mined
ordering, not recall over a fully adjudicated relevance set.
More importantly, its prerequisite width-7 bridge fails because the historical flattened/packed
path is materially batch dependent in the pinned stack. The current three-seed result is therefore
about recipes selected by that implementation. Corrected no-packing retraining in a new namespace is
required before generalizing the contrast to isolated eight-way dense-retriever training.

The temporal bridge uses nine branches and only three randomized order seeds, and its spectral
predictor is measured after optimizer assignment; leave-one-seed-out performance is therefore a
small-sample stress test rather than an identified mediator estimate. The 84-row forward bridge holds
out source runs but reuses the same 14 benchmark tasks, while the dose and band interventions change
components at frozen weights rather than training a controlled spectral operator. Even joint passage
supports causal-chain triangulation, not formal mediation of the full-training retrieval effect.

The Dense-only scope was adopted post hoc after some two-architecture results were visible. Its
operational motivation does not erase the risk of selective emphasis. We mitigate that risk by
retaining the complete LateOn provenance, labeling it exploratory, excluding it from all main
confidence intervals and architecture claims, and stating the amendment in the abstract, methods,
and artifact documentation.

\section{Ethical Considerations}

This study does not collect new human-subject data or deploy a retrieval system to users. It adapts
released models and datasets whose source corpora can nevertheless contain social biases, harmful
content, copyrighted material, or personal information. Higher benchmark nDCG is not evidence of
fairness, safety, privacy, or suitability for a particular deployment. A deployed retriever would
require corpus-specific auditing, filtering and access controls, and human review appropriate to its
users and failure costs.

We report useful wall time, throughput, and GPU-hours because an optimizer recommendation also has
resource consequences. Those measurements are specific to this hardware and software stack and do
not justify unbounded hyperparameter search. Any public artifact release must additionally respect
the licenses and usage terms of the upstream model, training sources, and evaluation datasets.

\section{Execution-Path Audit Details}
\label{sec:execution-audit-details}

All 224 queries and their positive-plus-seven-negative texts match the frozen validation source,
and checkpoint hashes match. Nevertheless, all 12 padded width-7 evaluations fail the predeclared
$10^{-5}$ reproduction tolerance; the largest sample/metric discrepancy is 8.286419. In a pinned
two-example control at one AdamW checkpoint, scoring an example in a packed pair versus by itself
changes a cosine score by as much as 0.211914. With flattening disabled, padded pair-versus-singleton
