Understanding Muon in Dense Retriever Adaptation}

\begin{abstract}
Dense retrievers are usually adapted with AdamW, whereas Muon changes the geometry of hidden-matrix
updates through approximate orthogonalization. It is unclear whether this inductive bias helps when
the starting encoder was itself pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer. We study AdamW, Muon, and
NorMuon on DenseOn using one deterministic \NumTrainingQueries-query training view, four learning
rates per optimizer, five training stages, and \NumBEIRTasks{} decontaminated zero-shot retrieval
tasks. The discovery results expose a local--global reversal: at common weights and matched update
norms, Muon-family directions need not improve the mean query margin as much as AdamW, while their
accumulated trajectories have higher one-seed, four-learning-rate-median final BEIR point estimates
in discovery. Their
immediate errors also redistribute rather than uniformly dominate AdamW's worst-query tail. We test
whether repeated, trajectory-changing adaptation---rather than a universally better one-step
direction---explains those discovery differences, using routing-matched AdamW, shared-start branches,
frozen spectrum-to-retrieval stress tests, and a three-seed confirmation. \DiscoveryHeadline
\ConfirmationHeadline The original project also ran
exploratory LateOn experiments; a user-directed, post-hoc scope amendment excludes them from all
primary and confirmatory inference and retains them only as historical supplementary material.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AdamW is the default optimizer for adapting text embedding models. Its coordinate-wise
second-moment normalization is effective, but it ignores the matrix organization of Transformer
projections. Muon instead approximately orthogonalizes momentum updates for hidden matrices, and
NorMuon follows that transform with neuron-wise adaptation
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. These matrix-aware rules have shown
promising efficiency in language-model pretraining. That result does not imply an advantage during
retrieval fine-tuning: the encoder begins at an Adam-pretrained solution, the training signal is a
contrastive ranking loss, and useful zero-shot rankings must be retained while new margins are
acquired.

Recent work makes this optimizer switch a genuine tension rather than a routine hyperparameter
choice. Muon can disrupt Adam-pretrained models as update strength grows, while optimizer
consistency can improve the learning--forgetting trade-off
\citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Conversely, Muon's higher-rank matrix directions could
spread adaptation over more features and avoid concentrating damage in a few high-leverage
directions. Dense retrieval offers a direct functional test: every update is ultimately judged by
query--document margins and rankings, not by its spectrum or training loss alone.

Our central observation is deliberately not ``Muon takes a better step.'' In the completed
discovery diagnostics, Frobenius-matched Muon-family virtual steps can be weaker than AdamW in mean
immediate margin improvement, yet the one-seed, four-learning-rate-median final BEIR point estimates
are 0.5901/0.5910 for Muon/NorMuon versus 0.5858 for AdamW. We call this tension the
\emph{local--global reversal}; it is an exploratory aggregate over the frozen grid, not the
validation-selected comparison. It rules out the simplest
one-step-descent explanation and moves the mechanism question to what repeated updates do: the first
step changes the parameters, which changes the next gradient, the set of difficult queries, and the
future optimizer state.

We therefore separate four objects that are often conflated: the update prescribed at the same
weights, the per-query distribution of its immediate effects, the accumulated trajectory induced by
repeated adaptation, and the final retrieval ranking. This separation also sharpens the robustness
question. A matrix-aware update can sacrifice average immediate progress without uniformly reducing
AdamW's worst errors; it may instead change which queries occupy the adverse tail. Whether this tail
redistribution becomes beneficial only after accumulation is a testable retrieval-specific claim,
not a restatement of Muon's definition.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A controlled DenseOn comparison of AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon over a complete learning-rate
    sweep, five-stage training dynamics, and a decontaminated BEIR evaluation, with optimizer-state,
    throughput, and time-to-quality reported separately.
    \item A common-state analysis that distinguishes definition-level spectral flattening from
    functional evidence and reveals a local--global reversal between matched one-step effects and
    accumulated retrieval outcomes.
    \item A query-level analysis showing that the relevant immediate phenomenon is tail
    redistribution rather than uniform Muon dominance, together with a frozen shared-start test of
    whether repeated updates improve the acquisition--preservation frontier.
    \item A falsifiable causal-chain test that requires early spectra to predict held-out branch
    outcomes beyond optimizer labels and norm controls, then requires fixed-state dose, band, basis,
    and held-run retrieval evidence. A completed failure is retained as a claimable negative result.
    \item Routing-matched hybrid AdamW that measures grouping and auxiliary-rate effects within
    AdamW and supplies a separately tuned, matched-routing comparator for Muon-family recipes,
    together with three new negative-sampling seeds that keep the final optimizer comparison
    independent of BEIR-based discovery selection.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Mechanistic Predictions}

\subsection{Three update rules}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise preconditioner,
\begin{equation}
\Delta W_t^{\mathrm{AdamW}} =
-\eta\frac{\widehat{M}_t}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}_t}+\epsilon}-\eta\lambda W_t.
\end{equation}
Muon instead applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum matrix. If
$M=U\Sigma V^\top$, the idealized transform is $\operatorname{polar}(M)=UV^\top$, approximately
equalizing nonzero singular values \citep{jordan2024muon}. The practical bfloat16 polynomial is an
approximation rather than an exact projection. NorMuon then forms a row-wise historical second
moment and rescales the orthogonalized update before restoring a matrix-level scale
\citep{li2025normuon}.

Spectral equalization is intrinsic to Muon's operator, and row-wise historical adaptation is
intrinsic to NorMuon. Neither fact establishes better retrieval. A flatter update can amplify weak
or noisy directions, and row balancing can change strongly without affecting a ranking
\citep{pang2026htmuon,qu2026finetune}. We accordingly treat spectra and row statistics as operator
fingerprints. They become explanatory only if a controlled change reaches margins or rankings and
persists under repeated updates.

\subsection{Local direction versus accumulated adaptation}

At parameters $\theta_t$, a same-state intervention compares operators on the same gradient history:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\theta_t' &= \theta_t + \alpha c_t^o\Delta\theta_t^o, \\
c_t^o &=
\frac{\lVert \Delta\theta_t^{\mathrm{AdamW}}\rVert_F}
     {\lVert \Delta\theta_t^{o}\rVert_F},
\quad o\in\{\mathrm{Muon},\mathrm{NorMuon}\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This identifies an immediate directional effect at a matched global or per-tensor budget. It does
not predict an entire trajectory, because committing the update changes the next gradient and each
optimizer's history. The local--global reversal therefore motivates an accumulated-adaptation
hypothesis: Muon-family directions may be locally conservative on average but steer subsequent
gradients toward a trajectory with better held-out margins and zero-shot rankings.

\subsection{Tail redistribution}

For a per-query loss change $\delta_i^o$, mean improvement and adverse-tail behavior are distinct.
We report fixed quantiles and symmetric cross-tails: the worst set selected under AdamW is evaluated
under every operator, and each Muon-family operator's own worst set is evaluated under AdamW. Low
overlap or opposite cross-tail orderings imply redistribution of fragile queries, not uniform tail
suppression. This distinction is central for DenseOn: a pooled representation can improve its mean
margin while moving failure mass among queries. The prospectively frozen shared-start endpoint asks
the stronger question of whether the three-seed median simultaneously improves query-disjoint loss
p95 and unseen margin p05 after repeated updates.

\paragraph{Causal-chain research question.}
Does a specific change in the singular-value distribution explain an accumulated functional effect
and predict a later retrieval change, rather than merely restating Muon's known spectral signature?
We require held-out prediction and interventions that can reject this explanation; no checkpoint
correlation, optimizer label, or fixed-state geometric fingerprint answers this question by itself.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Dense retrieval.}
Dense dual encoders compress queries and documents into single vectors and rank by their similarity.
BEIR established heterogeneous zero-shot retrieval as a demanding test of whether adaptation
preserves broadly useful ranking behavior \citep{thakur2021beir}. DenseOn provides an unsupervised,
not-yet-fine-tuned starting checkpoint with an open training recipe
\citep{sourty2026denseon}. Representation geometry has been linked to retrieval quality, including
work balancing isotropic and anisotropic components \citep{kim2024hil}; here, rank and isotropy are
possible bridge variables rather than endpoints.

\paragraph{Matrix-aware optimization and optimizer switching.}
Muon exploits hidden-matrix structure and has shown favorable scaling in language-model pretraining
\citep{liu2025muon}; NorMuon adds neuron-wise adaptation after orthogonalization
\citep{li2025normuon}. Concurrent studies warn that switching an Adam-pretrained model to Muon can
degrade downstream performance as update strength increases
\citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Other analyses connect Muon to larger margins and feature
rank \citep{ruan2026features}, or warn that spectral equalization can overemphasize noise-dominated
directions \citep{pang2026htmuon}. Newton--Muon highlights missing input-covariance preconditioning
\citep{du2026newtonmuon}, while adaptive Schatten geometry argues against a single matrix rule for
every layer \citep{massena2026schatten}. Our contribution is not another intrinsic description of
these operators; it is the controlled path from a same-state direction, through query-level effects
and accumulated adaptation, to dense retrieval rankings.

\section{Controlled Dense Retrieval Study}

\subsection{Scope amendment}

The project was originally designed as a two-architecture DenseOn/LateOn study. After the original
discovery runs and some exploratory mechanism outputs were visible, the user directing the project
requested that subsequent work focus on dense retrieval because LateOn training was substantially
slower and the intended paper audience placed less value on that architecture. This is a
\emph{user-directed, post-hoc scope amendment}, not a preregistered scientific exclusion. We preserve
the LateOn configurations, checkpoints, logs, and exploratory tables for audit, but do not pool them
with DenseOn, use them as replication, estimate architecture interactions from them, or let them
affect primary/confirmatory wording. All main-text hypotheses, accumulated branches, routing controls,
and three-seed inference below concern DenseOn only.

\subsection{Model, data, and objective}

DenseOn has undergone unsupervised retrieval pretraining but not the supervised adaptation studied
here \citep{sourty2026denseon}. We select one deterministic view of \NumTrainingQueries{} queries
from the released corpus. Each group contains one positive and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct sampled
negatives. We allocate queries proportionally over the seven source datasets, reject a candidate
unless its mined score is below $0.95$ times the positive score, and sample without replacement from
the first ten eligible candidates using stable seed-42 hashes. All discovery runs share group
identities and order. DenseOn uses cosine InfoNCE at temperature 0.02, without in-batch or
cross-device negatives, a maximum query/document context length of \ContextLength{}, global batch
size 128, one epoch, bfloat16 forward passes, FlashAttention, and gradient checkpointing.

\subsection{Discovery matrix and evaluation}

We train AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon at four learning rates each. AdamW uses
$\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$; Muon and NorMuon use
$\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$. Muon-family optimizers route hidden
two-dimensional Transformer matrices through the matrix rule and auxiliary parameters through
AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. We retain checkpoints at 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of training. Runs
use four GPUs, micro-batch 8 per GPU, four accumulation steps, 10\% linear warmup, linear decay, and
global gradient clipping at 1.0. The final partial accumulation is retained, so all 500K groups are
consumed in 3,907 optimizer steps.

Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same \NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite
\citep{thakur2021beir}. We report nDCG@10 against steps, examples, useful wall time, and GPU-hours,
and report peak memory and optimizer-state size separately. Full learning-rate trajectories are
retained; best-test-cell selection is never treated as confirmatory evidence.

\subsection{Selection and three-seed confirmation}

The discovery grid is exploratory. One recipe per optimizer is selected using a frozen 4,096-query
validation set disjoint from the 500K training ledger and without access to BEIR scores. The rule
first minimizes eight-way validation loss, then maximizes positive margin, with the smaller learning
rate as the final tie-breaker. Three new seeds preserve query and positive identities while
independently resampling negatives from reconstructed candidate pools. Only these new-seed DenseOn
runs support the confirmatory AdamW--Muon and AdamW--NorMuon comparisons.

We independently resample seeds and tasks in a deterministic 20,000-draw hierarchical bootstrap and
report nominal and familywise 95\% intervals for the two primary optimizer contrasts. The Bonferroni
interval retains all six comparisons fixed before the post-hoc Dense-only scope amendment, even
though only DenseOn rows enter the paper. Positive, negative, or inconclusive headline wording is
determined only after the frozen three-seed matrix is complete. Aggregate MTEB outputs do not contain
per-query rankings, so we make no query-level significance claim for BEIR.

\subsection{Frozen causal-chain protocol}

The shared-start temporal test uses all 45 adjacent displacements from nine randomized branches and
five checkpoints, with the first displacement measured from the common start. For each challenger,
the primary predictor is hidden-matrix tail singular-value energy averaged over stages 1--2; the
outcomes are stage-5 query-disjoint loss p95 and unseen margin p05. We fit paired-contrast ordinary
least squares while leaving one complete order seed out. The reference is an optimizer-label-only
mean fit on the two training seeds; total hidden-update and current-weight Frobenius norms are frozen
negative controls. Stable rank, entropy rank, other spectral bands, and row imbalance are reported as
secondaries but cannot rescue the primary test.

Temporal support requires all five frozen conditions: (i) both challenger predictor shifts are
positive in at least two of three seeds; (ii) both final outcomes favor each challenger in at least
two seeds; (iii) the primary predictor improves pooled leave-one-seed-out RMSE over optimizer labels
for both outcomes; (iv) neither norm control equals or exceeds that improvement for either outcome;
and (v) adding the primary predictor does not increase either absolute challenger coefficient for
either outcome. Failure of any condition is a complete negative temporal result, not missing evidence.

The fixed-state test interpolates the Muon spectrum into the AdamW basis at
$\lambda\in\{0,.25,.5,.75,1\}$, with every direction Frobenius-matched. Both loss-p95 and margin-p05
responses must be weakly monotone at at least eight of ten frozen anchors; the prespecified tail band
must beat the head and middle bands, and the spectrum transplant must beat the matched basis-swap
control, each on both metrics at at least eight anchors. Finally, 84 observations join 14 tasks to
the two eligible next-checkpoint transitions in each of three source runs. Leaving one full source
run out, a spectrum-based immediate effect must improve same-task next-checkpoint nDCG@10 RMSE beyond
task and transition effects, while its basis-control counterpart must not match that improvement.
Only joint passage of dose, band, basis, and held-run bridge rules supports the spectral-component
account.

\section{From Same-State Updates to Query Effects}

\subsection{Common-state update fingerprints}

At the pretrained state and selected 20, 60, and 100\% DenseOn checkpoints, we freeze weights and
replay the same ordered sequence of eight gradients into each optimizer. This frozen-weight state
warm-up is the formal common-state protocol; one-gradient cold starts are auxiliary. Weight decay is
kept separate, and directions are compared raw and after global or per-tensor Frobenius matching.
Primary measurements are effective rank, condition number, row-energy concentration, layer
allocation, and pairwise angle. \CommonStateHeadline

\input{generated/common-state}

These measurements verify that the implementations impose different update geometry. They cannot
explain retrieval by themselves. In particular, a higher stable rank is an operator fingerprint,
whereas a margin or ranking change under a matched intervention is functional evidence. The complete
function-preserving coordinate diagnostic appears in Appendix~\ref{tab:basis-sensitivity-results}.

\subsection{Matched one-step intervention and the local disadvantage}

At each common-state anchor, we intervene along AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon directions while holding
weights, examples, gradient history, and update norm fixed. Baseline, three positive scales, and a
sign-reversed condition are evaluated on the same unseen queries. The completed discovery diagnostic
shows the key local disadvantage: Muon-family directions do not exceed AdamW's mean immediate margin
gain under the largest frozen Frobenius-matched step. Because this is a fixed-state intervention, it
identifies a directional difference; because it is one step, it does not identify the long-horizon
effect of committing that direction.

\subsection{Post-hoc state-feedback evidence}

A post-hoc common-state synthesis provides a more specific candidate explanation. The AdamW--Muon
update cosine decreases from the pretrained anchor along both AdamW- and Muon-induced trajectories,
whereas Muon and NorMuon remain nearly collinear with one another. At the final anchors, the two
matrix-aware directions also align less with the same terminal gradient than AdamW does. Together
with the worse Frobenius-matched immediate mean margin but higher one-seed,
four-learning-rate-median discovery BEIR point estimates, this pattern is consistent with
\emph{optimizer-induced state feedback}: committing an operator's
direction moves DenseOn into states that elicit different future gradients, so the accumulated path
cannot be reduced to the quality of one frozen-state step. The cosine synthesis was defined after
inspection and is descriptive. Exact values enter only through the audited result table
(Table~\ref{tab:common-state-results}); shared-start branches test the prospective accumulation
hypothesis, while the frozen spectrum/basis transplant distinguishes a
singular-value account from a singular-vector-basis account.

\subsection{Tail redistribution, not uniform dominance}

Mean effects conceal which queries regress. We therefore report loss p95/p99, margin p01/p05, and
symmetric worst-tail comparisons selected separately under AdamW and each Muon-family rule. In the
DenseOn discovery anchors, Muon and NorMuon improve outcomes on AdamW's worst set while AdamW can do
better on each challenger's own worst set, with limited overlap between selected sets. This pattern
is tail redistribution: the operator changes the identity of fragile queries rather than uniformly
suppressing one shared tail. It is descriptive of the inspected anchors and is not yet a robustness
claim.

For any matrix direction $D$,
$\lVert D\rVert_2=\lVert D\rVert_F/\sqrt{\operatorname{srank}(D)}$. Under per-tensor Frobenius
matching, a higher stable rank entails lower maximum singular amplification, and
$\lVert Dh\rVert_2\leq\lVert D\rVert_2\lVert h\rVert_2$. This supplies a candidate link between
update spectrum and extreme feature changes, but it does not prove that the spectrum causes the
observed tail membership. The frozen spectrum/basis transplant tests that attribution; it remains a
fixed-state explanation even if successful.
The full cross-tail, accumulated-tail, and spectrum-versus-basis readouts are reported in Appendix
Tables~\ref{tab:tail-identity-results}--\ref{tab:spectral-tail-results}.

\subsection{Routing-matched AdamW}

Native AdamW applies one learning rate to every trainable parameter, whereas Muon-family runs route
hidden matrices through one rule and auxiliary parameters through AdamW. A hybrid AdamW control uses
the identical partition and auxiliary learning rate. Native AdamW versus hybrid AdamW therefore
measures routing and auxiliary-rate effects within AdamW at each shared AdamW learning rate. Hybrid
AdamW versus Muon matches routing and the auxiliary rate, but each rule retains its independently
swept learning-rate range; it compares separately tuned recipes rather than identifying
orthogonalization, the matrix rule, or update scale alone. A full-length scale-matched component
control would be needed for that attribution. The present control removes a routing confound from
the recipe comparison but is not by itself a causal decomposition of Muon.

\subsection{Representation and score geometry}

On fixed in-distribution and unseen probes, we measure the full query-margin distribution, embedding
effective rank and isotropy, hubness, and top-$k$ agreement with the pretrained model. We join these
quantities to DenseOn trajectories and separately analyze within-run first differences.

\section{Discussion}

The local--global reversal changes what a successful explanation must show. If the three-seed
retrieval advantage and the shared-start endpoint both hold, the result cannot be explained by
better immediate mean descent. The supported account would instead be that Muon-family updates alter
future gradients and redistribute query risk, producing a better trajectory after accumulation. If
hybrid AdamW matches Muon, the routing/auxiliary-rate recipe is sufficient to reproduce the observed
difference within this grid; that does not identify the matrix operator as the alternative cause. If
the shared-start tail endpoint fails, the local redistribution remains real but cannot explain the
final gain. If the three-seed interval is inconclusive, the mechanism study constrains explanations
of the discovery
effect without establishing a general recipe advantage.

The causal-chain verdict further separates an optimizer outcome from its explanation. If the temporal
or fixed-state chain is negative, Muon's known spectral flattening does not explain the retrieval
result tested here, even if the optimizer comparison itself is positive. If every frozen component
passes, the evidence supports a spectral-component chain across randomized branches, fixed-state
interventions, and held-run prediction, but still does not identify formal mediation of the final
BEIR contrast.

This conditional structure also prevents definition-level properties from masquerading as novelty.
Muon's spectral flattening and NorMuon's row adaptation are known features of the algorithms. The
retrieval-specific contribution is evidence about whether a locally weaker direction changes which
queries fail, whether that effect accumulates from a shared checkpoint, and whether the resulting
trajectory improves decontaminated retrieval under new negative-sampling seeds.

Practical recommendations therefore depend on the completed controls. Muon is recommended over
AdamW only if it survives routing matching, accumulated shared-start testing, and familywise
three-seed confirmation; otherwise the paper reports a conditional or negative result rather than a
universal winner.

\section{Limitations}

Primary inference concerns one 149M-parameter DenseOn checkpoint, one English supervised corpus,
and one epoch of full-parameter adaptation. It does not establish behavior at other scales,
languages, objectives, pooling schemes, or sparse and late-interaction retrievers. The base model was
pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer, so the study tests optimizer switching rather than
Muon-from-scratch pretraining. Four learning rates provide a broad but finite search, and three
confirmation seeds may leave small effects uncertain. Intrinsic geometry metrics are not causal by
themselves; causal language is restricted to fixed-state interventions and shared-start branches.
Decontamination reduces known overlap but cannot prove the absence of every semantic duplicate.

The temporal bridge uses nine branches and only three randomized order seeds, and its spectral
predictor is measured after optimizer assignment; leave-one-seed-out performance is therefore a
small-sample stress test rather than an identified mediator estimate. The 84-row forward bridge holds
out source runs but reuses the same 14 benchmark tasks, while the dose and band interventions change
components at frozen weights rather than training a controlled spectral operator. Even joint passage
supports causal-chain triangulation, not formal mediation of the full-training retrieval effect.

The Dense-only scope was adopted post hoc after some two-architecture results were visible. Its
operational motivation does not erase the risk of selective emphasis. We mitigate that risk by
retaining the complete LateOn provenance, labeling it exploratory, excluding it from all main
confidence intervals and architecture claims, and stating the amendment in the abstract, methods,
and artifact documentation.

\section{Ethics and Reproducibility}

The experiments adapt openly released retrieval models and datasets. We preserve frozen task,
checkpoint, and learning-rate cells, including failures, together with model revisions, dataset
fingerprints, negative-sampling seeds, package versions, checkpoint hashes, and W\&B histories. The
confirmatory seed and validation selection protocols remain separate from the exploratory BEIR
matrix. Released code supports strict artifact audits before producing tables or figures. Historical
LateOn artifacts are retained rather than deleted after the scope amendment.
