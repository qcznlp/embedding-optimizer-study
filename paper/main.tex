\title{From Update Geometry to Retrieval Geometry:\\
Understanding Muon for Dense and Late-Interaction Text Embeddings}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{abstract}
Neural retrievers are usually adapted with AdamW, whereas matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon
promise faster and more robust optimization by changing the geometry of matrix updates. Whether
that inductive bias transfers to Adam-pretrained embedding models---and whether it affects dense and
late-interaction retrievers through the same mechanism---remains unclear. We conduct a controlled
\NumDiscoveryRuns-run study over two retriever architectures, three optimizers, four learning rates,
five training stages, and \NumBEIRTasks{} decontaminated zero-shot retrieval tasks. We then evaluate
every optimizer at common weight states and on common gradient histories, relate update spectra and
row balance to representation rank, margins, ranking drift, and MaxSim token utilization, and test
the resulting links with scale-matched interventions, shared-start branches, and a parameter-routing
control. \DiscoveryHeadline. \ConfirmationHeadline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AdamW is the default optimizer for training and adapting text embedding models. Its coordinate-wise
second-moment normalization is effective and familiar, but it ignores the matrix structure of
Transformer projections. Muon instead orthogonalizes momentum updates for hidden matrices, and
NorMuon augments that transform with neuron-wise adaptation
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. Large-scale language-model pretraining
suggests that these matrix-aware rules can improve compute efficiency. It does not follow that they
will improve retrieval fine-tuning: the starting model was pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer,
the contrastive objective depends on rankings rather than next-token likelihood, and dense and
late-interaction models expose different functional bottlenecks.

Recent full-fine-tuning studies sharpen this uncertainty into a competing hypothesis. Switching an
Adam-pretrained model to Muon can degrade downstream performance, with the gap increasing as the
update grows, while preserving the pretraining optimizer can improve the learning--forgetting
tradeoff \citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Retrieval is a useful stress test of that claim:
adaptation must separate new query--document pairs while preserving broadly useful zero-shot
rankings. We therefore do not assume that Muon's better-conditioned updates are beneficial. We test
whether faster acquisition and pretrained-function preservation can coexist, and whether any
tradeoff differs between a single-vector bottleneck and token-level MaxSim.

This paper therefore asks a mechanism question rather than staging another optimizer leaderboard:
\emph{how does matrix-aware optimization change the path from an Adam-pretrained encoder to a
retrieval function, and when are those changes useful?} We separate four spaces that are often
conflated: the update prescribed at the same parameters, the accumulated weight trajectory, the
resulting representation and score geometry, and the final retrieval ranking. This separation is
essential because large parameter-space distances can be functionally redundant, while small
directional changes can alter nearest-neighbor rankings.

Our study uses the unsupervised DenseOn and LateOn checkpoints
\citep{sourty2026denseon}, a shared deterministic \NumTrainingQueries-query training view with
\NumHardNegatives{} explicit negatives per query, and no in-batch negatives. The discovery stage
retains \NumDiscoveryCheckpoints{} checkpoints and evaluates all \NumDiscoveryUnits{}
checkpoint--task combinations. A second stage holds weights, batches, gradients, optimizer-state
history, and update budgets fixed to identify optimizer-specific update fingerprints. Finally,
functional interventions and short shared-start branches test whether these local fingerprints
survive into retrieval behavior.

Our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We provide a controlled dense/late-interaction benchmark of AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon,
    reporting learning dynamics, time-to-quality, memory, learning-rate tolerance, and complete
    decontaminated retrieval results.
    \item We separate definition-level optimizer fingerprints from explanatory evidence, and test a
    local-to-global reversal in which scale-matched Muon-family steps need not be locally superior
    even when their accumulated trajectories yield better retrieval geometry.
    \item We connect update geometry to retrieval-specific function geometry: dense-vector rank,
    hubness, and margins, plus token-level MaxSim evidence concentration for late interaction.
    \item We isolate update rule, parameter routing, update magnitude, and optimizer-pretraining
    mismatch through hybrid AdamW, scale-matched virtual steps, shared-checkpoint branches, and
    prospectively frozen multi-seed confirmation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Predictions}

\subsection{Three update geometries}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise preconditioner,
\begin{equation}
\Delta W_t^{\mathrm{AdamW}} =
-\eta\frac{\widehat{M}_t}{\sqrt{\widehat{V}_t}+\epsilon}-\eta\lambda W_t,
\end{equation}
whereas Muon applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum matrix,
approximately replacing its nonzero singular values by a common scale \citep{jordan2024muon}.
More precisely, if $M=U\Sigma V^\top$, the idealized transform is
$\operatorname{polar}(M)=UV^\top$; the practical five-step bfloat16 polynomial is an approximation,
not an exact projection. Spectral equalization is therefore intrinsic to Muon's operator, whereas
high row-norm variance is not a universal mathematical consequence. An exact full-row-rank polar
factor has equal row norms when $M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $m\leq n$; for $m>n$, row norms are
leverage scores and may differ, and approximate orthogonalization can reintroduce variation in either
shape.

NorMuon subsequently forms an exponential moving average of each row's squared magnitude and applies
the resulting row-wise preconditioner before restoring a matrix-level update scale
\citep{li2025normuon}. Thus neuron-wise scale adaptation is intrinsic to NorMuon, but exact row
equality is not: the historical second moment need not equal the current row energy. Diagonal row
scaling can also perturb Muon's singular spectrum. Consequently, we predict that Muon increases the
effective rank and improves the conditioning of individual matrix updates relative to AdamW, while
NorMuon reduces dispersion and concentration of row energy relative to Muon without necessarily
preserving every spectral property. A flatter update spectrum is not assumed to be beneficial: it
may amplify noise-dominated directions or disrupt useful Adam-pretrained features
\citep{pang2026htmuon,qu2026finetune}.

\subsection{Optimizer mismatch as a competing hypothesis}

The update-conditioning and optimizer-mismatch accounts make different predictions. If Muon's
matrix preconditioner primarily improves optimization, it should reach a fixed retrieval quality in
fewer examples or less useful wall time without necessarily moving farther from the pretrained
function. If optimizer mismatch dominates, stronger Muon updates may lower supervised loss while
increasing drift from pretrained rankings and degrading zero-shot retrieval. We distinguish these
possibilities by reporting learning and preservation signals separately: contrastive loss and
validation margins measure acquisition, while decontaminated BEIR, pretrained top-$k$ agreement,
and score drift measure retained retrieval behavior. Common-state scale matching and shared-start
branches then separate direction from update magnitude; parameter distance alone cannot adjudicate
between the two accounts.

\subsection{From update geometry to retrieval geometry}

The hypothesized chain is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{operator} &\rightarrow \text{update/trajectory} \\
                &\rightarrow \text{representation/score} \\
                &\rightarrow \text{ranking}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Dense retrieval compresses a sequence into one vector, so we measure embedding covariance rank,
isotropy, hubness, and query--positive margins. LateOn uses a ColBERT-style MaxSim interaction
\citep{khattab2020colbert}; we additionally measure how evenly query tokens contribute evidence and
how broadly document tokens are selected. A geometry metric is explanatory only when it predicts a
margin or ranking change, and checkpoint-level correlations remain observational.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Neural retrieval.}
Dense dual encoders score a query and document through compact single-vector representations,
whereas ColBERT-style retrievers preserve token vectors and aggregate token-wise maxima
\citep{khattab2020colbert}. BEIR established heterogeneous zero-shot evaluation as a central stress
test for retrievers \citep{thakur2021beir}; DenseOn and LateOn provide unusually controlled dense
and late-interaction starting points trained from an open recipe \citep{sourty2026denseon}.
Representation geometry has already been connected to zero-shot retrieval---for example, HIL
explicitly balances isotropic and anisotropic components \citep{kim2024hil}. We treat isotropy and
effective rank as explanatory variables rather than contributions by themselves, and require them
to connect optimizer interventions to scores or rankings.

\paragraph{Matrix-aware optimization.}
AdamW combines coordinate-wise moment adaptation with decoupled weight decay
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}. Muon instead exploits the matrix organization of hidden weights and
has shown favorable scaling in language-model pretraining \citep{liu2025muon}. NorMuon identifies
non-uniform row norms after orthogonalization and adds neuron-wise second-moment adaptation
\citep{li2025normuon}. Concurrent analyses of Adam-pretrained model adaptation report that a naive
optimizer switch can disrupt pretrained knowledge as update strength grows, and that
optimizer--model consistency can improve the learning--forgetting tradeoff
\citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Other concurrent work supplies competing explanations:
Muon-pretrained features can have larger margins and effective rank \citep{ruan2026features}, while
heavy-tailed spectral analyses warn that orthogonalization can overemphasize noise directions
\citep{pang2026htmuon}. Newton--Muon interprets standard Muon as omitting input-covariance
preconditioning \citep{du2026newtonmuon}, and adaptive Schatten geometry argues against one fixed
matrix rule for every layer \citep{massena2026schatten}. These works motivate our hypotheses, but
none traces the complete path from same-state updates through dense and token-level retrieval
rankings. Our study instead tests that path during full-parameter contrastive adaptation, where the
target and retained capabilities are both ranking functions and late interaction exposes
token-level evidence allocation.

\section{Controlled Retrieval Study}

\subsection{Models, data, and objective}

DenseOn is a single-vector encoder and LateOn is a token-level late-interaction encoder. Both base
checkpoints have undergone unsupervised retrieval pretraining but not the supervised fine-tuning
studied here \citep{sourty2026denseon}. We select one deterministic view of
\NumTrainingQueries{} queries from the released training corpus. Every group contains one positive
and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct sampled negatives; all runs share the exact group identities and
order. We allocate queries proportionally over the seven source datasets, reject a mined candidate
unless its score is below $0.95$ times the positive score, and sample seven without replacement from
the first ten eligible candidates using stable seed-42 hashes. DenseOn uses cosine InfoNCE at
temperature 0.02. LateOn uses normalized token vectors and MeanMaxSim at temperature 0.001. Neither
objective uses in-batch or cross-device negatives. Both use a maximum query/document context length
of \ContextLength{}, a global batch size of 128, one epoch, bfloat16 forward passes, FlashAttention,
and gradient checkpointing.

\subsection{Discovery matrix}

For each architecture, we train AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon at four learning rates. AdamW uses
$\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$; Muon and NorMuon use
$\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$. Muon-family optimizers route hidden
two-dimensional Transformer matrices through the matrix rule and all auxiliary parameters through
AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. We retain checkpoints at 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of training.
All runs use four GPUs, micro-batch 8 per GPU, four accumulation steps, a 10\% linear warmup followed
by linear decay, and global gradient clipping at 1.0. The final partial accumulation is retained, so
all 500K groups are consumed exactly once in 3,907 optimizer steps.

All checkpoints are evaluated on the same \NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite
\citep{thakur2021beir}. We report nDCG@10 against steps, examples, useful wall time, and GPU-hours;
peak memory and optimizer-state size are reported separately. Full learning-rate trajectories are
shown, not only each optimizer's best point.

\subsection{Selection and confirmation}

The discovery grid is exploratory. We select one recipe per architecture and optimizer using a
frozen 4,096-query validation set that is disjoint from the 500K training ledger and does not use
BEIR scores. The selection rule minimizes eight-way validation loss, then maximizes positive margin,
with the smaller learning rate as the final tie-breaker. Three new seeds preserve query and positive
identities while independently resampling negatives from the reconstructed candidate pools. Only
these new-seed runs support the confirmatory optimizer comparison.

For confirmation, we independently resample seeds and tasks in a deterministic 20,000-draw
hierarchical bootstrap. We report both nominal 95\% intervals and Bonferroni familywise 95\%
intervals over the six prespecified family-by-optimizer contrasts. Positive, negative, and
inconclusive headline language is determined only by the familywise interval. The retained aggregate
MTEB outputs do not contain per-query rankings, so we make no query-level significance claim.

\section{What Do the Optimizers Change?}

\subsection{Integrated weight trajectories}

We stream all 120 checkpoints and analyze the 88 hidden matrices routed through Muon-family rules
separately from embeddings, norms, biases, and heads. For every tensor we measure displacement from
the pretrained state, row and column energy balance, concentration, and the coarse path joining the
pretrained state to the five retained checkpoints. Singular-spectrum fields are deliberately
disabled in this all-checkpoint tier; exact spectra are reserved for a frozen state--layer subset in
the common-state analysis.

Across \NumWeightPairs{} matched Muon/NorMuon checkpoint pairs, the NorMuon-to-Muon reference
displacement ratio lies in \DisplacementRatioRange. Despite this nearly identical aggregate scale,
the corresponding row-norm coefficient-of-variation ratio is \RowCVRatioRange, and the top-1\%-row
energy ratio is \TopRowEnergyRatioRange. The direction repeats at all five stages, four nominal
learning rates, and both architectures. This is evidence that NorMuon redistributes trajectory
energy across output rows without primarily changing total displacement; it is not yet evidence
that the redistribution causes a retrieval improvement.

\subsection{Common-state update fingerprints}

At the pretrained state and selected 20, 60, and 100\% checkpoints, we freeze weights and replay the
same ordered sequence of eight gradients into every optimizer. This frozen-weight state warm-up is
the formal common-state protocol; any one-gradient cold-start diagnostic is labeled auxiliary rather
than pooled with it. We keep weight decay separate and evaluate raw and per-tensor Frobenius-matched
directions. The primary measurements are effective rank, condition number, row-energy concentration,
layer allocation, and pairwise update angle. \CommonStateHeadline.

\paragraph{Function-preserving basis diagnostic.}
An arbitrary head rotation is not generally a symmetry of rotary position embeddings. We instead
apply seeded SO(2) rotations within each split-half RoPE plane, using the same rotation for query and
key and leaving value coordinates fixed. These transforms commute with RoPE and preserve every
attention logit; we verify this numerically in float64 at both model rope bases. At all 20
common-state anchors, we replay original and transformed gradient histories for QKV matrices at
layers 0, 10, and 21, inverse-map the prescribed updates, and measure direction, norm,
predicted-descent, and selected-head spectrum error. This appendix diagnostic tests coordinate
equivariance of the implemented optimizer \citep{singh2026basis} and is not treated as a retrieval
intervention. In exact arithmetic, the polar map and its Newton--Schulz matrix polynomial are
equivariant to orthogonal changes of basis on either side. AdamW's coordinate-wise second moment is
not generally equivariant to dense rotations. NorMuon's row-diagonal state also selects an output
coordinate system, so it retains right-orthogonal equivariance but not arbitrary left-orthogonal
equivariance. Its measured near-equivariance below is therefore specific to the frozen,
function-preserving RoPE transformations rather than a universal property.

\section{Causal Controls}

\paragraph{Routing-matched AdamW.}
The main AdamW sweep applies one learning rate to every trainable parameter, whereas Muon-family
runs use an auxiliary AdamW route. An eight-run hybrid AdamW control adopts the identical partition
and auxiliary rate, isolating the matrix transform from parameter routing.

\paragraph{Scale-matched virtual steps.}
At each common-state anchor, we intervene along AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon directions while holding
weights, examples, gradients, and update norm fixed. Baseline, three positive scales, and a
sign-reversed condition are evaluated on the same 224 unseen queries. These one-step results identify
immediate directional effects but not long-horizon training behavior.

After the complete discovery, common-state, and representation matrices were visible, we declared a
post-hoc local-to-global analysis that compares the largest frozen virtual-step condition with
final-stage medians over all four learning rates. It asks whether the ordering of immediate unseen
margin changes agrees with the ordering of accumulated unseen margins and BEIR scores. The same
analysis compares the independently validation-selected recipe with the within-optimizer discovery
BEIR oracle, which remains descriptive and never replaces the prospectively selected confirmation
recipe. This synthesis cannot establish a spectral mechanism by itself.

We additionally inspect the per-query distribution hidden by the mean intervention effect. This
mean--tail diagnostic is explicitly post hoc for the discovery anchors: it contrasts mean and fifth
percentile margin changes with the 95th and 99th percentiles of loss change. A secondary,
post-inspection diagnostic selects each operator's own worst loss tail and compares effects on both
sets, separating shared-tail severity suppression from a change in tail membership. Before any
shared-start outcome existed, we froze a separate final-stage rule requiring both lower
query-disjoint loss-tail and higher unseen margin-tail under the three-seed median. Thus the discovery
diagnostic can suggest a functional robustness account, while only the accumulated branch can test
whether it persists.

This tail result gives a more specific optimization hypothesis than ``Muon has flatter updates.''
For any matrix direction $D$,
$\lVert D\rVert_2=\lVert D\rVert_F/\sqrt{\operatorname{srank}(D)}$; hence, under our per-tensor
Frobenius match, the measured higher stable rank of the Muon-family direction entails lower maximum
singular amplification. Together with
$\lVert D h\rVert_2\leq\lVert D\rVert_2\lVert h\rVert_2$, this suggests that AdamW can achieve
better mean immediate descent by concentrating change in a few high-leverage directions while
producing larger regressions for aligned queries or tokens. Muon may instead trade mean progress for
a smaller adverse tail. Late interaction supplies a plausible architectural amplifier because a
token-wise maximum exposes the score to extreme token perturbations; the pooled dense score need not
preserve the same fragile-query set. Aggregate token-coverage and evidence-concentration probes do
not consistently distinguish the tuned optimizers, so they do not support a simpler ``uses more
tokens'' account.

Before any spectrum/basis-transplant output existed, we froze a tail-specific amendment: loss
p95/p99, margin p01/p05, and independently selected worst tails for AdamW and each transplanted
condition. If the Adam-basis/Muon-spectrum cell inherits the LateOn tail effect but the reciprocal
Muon-basis/Adam-spectrum cell does not, the immediate effect follows singular values rather than
their basis. This remains a fixed-state causal attribution, not a long-horizon mediation result.
More broadly, the tuned discovery point estimates show that an Adam-to-Muon switch need not always
hurt retrieval even though prior full-rank studies find optimizer mismatch
\citep{qu2026finetune}; the potentially novel claim is an architecture- and update-strength-dependent
interior regime, pending multi-seed confirmation, rather than mismatch or spectral flattening alone.

\paragraph{Shared-start branches.}
Both architectures branch from the same 60\% AdamW checkpoint. AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon receive
hidden learning rates calibrated to a common global $\lVert\Delta W\rVert_F/\lVert W\rVert_F$
target and train over the same 50K groups under three order seeds. All five checkpoints are scored
on the query-disjoint and unseen functional probes. \InterventionHeadline.

\section{Discussion}

The interpretation is selected by evidence rather than by optimizer preference. Definition-level
spectral flattening and row balancing validate the implementations but are not explanatory results.
A Muon time-to-quality gain with tied final retrieval would establish an optimization benefit but
not a better representation. A local-to-global reversal would instead rule out the simplest
``better one-step descent'' account and make the changing gradient trajectory the object requiring
explanation. A mean--tail trade-off would sharpen this account: the matrix-aware step could sacrifice
average immediate progress while avoiding severe query-level regressions, allowing moderate repeated
steps to acquire useful margins without erasing as much pretrained behavior. This functional tail
claim is distinct from merely observing Muon's definition-level spectral flattening and must survive
the frozen shared-start rule. The exploratory cross-tail result further suggests different failure
geometry across architectures: tail redistribution for the dense retriever but suppression of a
largely shared fragile-query tail for late interaction. Because that comparison was defined after
inspection, it is hypothesis-generating. Lower validation loss with worse BEIR performance would
locate that trajectory on an
acquisition--preservation frontier and support optimizer mismatch and functional drift. A NorMuon
advantage concentrated in LateOn, accompanied by improved token-evidence coverage, would support a
retrieval-specific role for row adaptation. If row geometry changes strongly without a corresponding
margin or ranking advantage, row balancing is a genuine optimizer effect but not the mediator of
retrieval quality.

The practical recommendation therefore conditions on model family, update strength, and the
desired compute/quality operating point rather than naming a universal winner. \ConfirmationHeadline.

\section{Limitations}

The study uses two related 149M-parameter base models and one English supervised corpus, so it does
not establish behavior at substantially different scales, languages, or objectives. Both starting
models were pretrained with an Adam-family optimizer, making the conclusions directly relevant to
optimizer switching but not to Muon-from-scratch pretraining. Without otherwise matched
Muon-pretrained DenseOn/LateOn checkpoints, the study can measure the consequences of switching but
cannot estimate a pretraining-optimizer-by-fine-tuning-optimizer interaction. Four learning rates
provide a broad but finite search, and three confirmation seeds may leave small effects uncertain. Intrinsic
geometry metrics are not causal by themselves; causal language is restricted to fixed-state
interventions and shared-start branches. Finally, decontamination reduces known overlap but cannot
prove the absence of every semantic duplicate.

\section{Ethics and Reproducibility}

The experiments adapt openly released retrieval models and datasets. We report all frozen task,
checkpoint, and learning-rate cells, including failures, and preserve model revisions, dataset
fingerprints, negative-sampling seeds, package versions, checkpoint hashes, and W\&B histories. The
confirmatory seed and recipe-selection protocol is kept separate from the exploratory BEIR matrix.
The released code supports strict artifact audits before producing paper tables or figures.
