\begin{abstract}
Does an optimizer help retrieval through the weight states it reaches or the updates it applies?
We compare AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon from one pretrained DenseOn checkpoint on 500,000 shared
queries, following 12 configurations through five checkpoints and 14 decontaminated BEIR tasks.
\CorrectedAbstractFinding{} Weight-space maps reveal distinct trajectories, but coordinate-use
and held-out-rate prediction tests do not establish a recipe-robust geometric explanation.
Crossing two fixed reached states with reset continuation rules separates the source-state
comparison from the subsequent update-rule comparison.
\StateOperatorAbstractFinding{}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Dense retrieval adapts a pretrained encoder so that a small vector preserves distinctions needed
to rank a large corpus. DenseOn's published adaptation recipe uses AdamW
\citep{sourty2026denseon}, whose coordinate-wise scaling differs from Muon's approximately
orthogonalized matrix updates \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon}. Changing that rule changes which weight states the
encoder reaches. The central question is whether the resulting motion creates more useful
retrieval representations, rather than simply spreading parameter changes across more directions.

There are reasons to expect either outcome. In pretraining studies, Muon learns features with
higher effective rank and stronger transfer \citep{ruan2026features}. In contrast, studies of
Adam-pretrained models find that switching optimizers during fine-tuning can disrupt learned
knowledge, particularly as update strength increases \citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}.
These findings concern different training settings. Neither tells us how an Adam-pretrained dense
retriever's weight space should change to improve corpus ranking.

Retrieval also exposes a distinction between spreading a representation and using it well.
Randomly removing many embedding coordinates can have surprisingly little effect, and removing
some coordinates can even improve retrieval \citep{takeshita2025dimensions}. Consequently, a
broader spectrum need not mean that more dimensions carry useful ranking evidence. We ask whether
optimizer-induced weight changes alter the balance and distribution of helpful and degrading
coordinate contributions.

We follow this question from weights to function. Starting from the same DenseOn checkpoint, we
train AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon on identical 500,000-query groups with seven hard negatives each.
Four rates per optimizer and five checkpoints expose the rate dependence and timing of retrieval
changes on 14 decontaminated BEIR tasks. We then trace the reached weight states and measure how
coordinate removal changes their representations' ranking behavior. To distinguish a useful
retrieval descriptor from a statistic that merely identifies the optimizer, we test prediction at
held-out learning-rate doses.

Figure~\ref{fig:weight-space-map} shows this progression. A separate $2\times2$ continuation
crosses two fixed reached states with reset AdamW or Muon. Calibrating update scale on a common
probe lets us ask whether further adaptation depends on both the starting weights and the
continuation rule. This intervention concerns whole states and subsequent optimization; it does
not isolate any particular geometric feature as the cause of the original retrieval difference.

Our results separate reached-state quality from continuation preference. Muon-family recipes
reach visibly different weight states; validation-selected retrieval gains do not become a
conclusive all-rate advantage. A native-coordinate participation difference changes sign under
shared rotations, and no tested state descriptor meets the predictive criterion across all four recipe
comparators. Yet the crossed continuation retains a positive average Muon-source state contrast
while its reset-Muon operator contrast is negative. Thus a useful reached state need not favor
the rule that produced it. This bounded result motivates studying optimizer-dependent weight
states without attributing their quality to an unverified spectral or dimension-use mechanism.

\section{From Optimizer Updates to Retrieval States}

\subsection{From weight-space motion to ranking evidence}

Let $e_\theta(x)$ be the unit-normalized embedding produced by weights $\theta$.
For a relevant document $d^+$ and a competing document $d^-$, write
$\delta_\theta=e_\theta(d^+)-e_\theta(d^-)$. Their cosine margin for query $q$ is
\begin{align}
m_\theta(q,d^+,d^-)
&=s_\theta(q,d^+)-s_\theta(q,d^-) \nonumber\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{768} e_{\theta,j}(q)\,\delta_{\theta,j}.
\label{eq:retrieval-margin}
\end{align}
This identity separates the spread of a representation from its alignment with relevance:
different coordinates may reinforce or oppose a ranking distinction. The encoder maps weight
changes into these query--document relationships, so a weight-displacement spectrum alone does
not determine whether the changed directions help retrieval.

Our analysis therefore distinguishes three objects: motion in parameter space, the allocation of
functional coordinate contributions in the reached representation, and full-corpus ranking.
Coordinate deletion measures sensitivity of that representation; because deletion is followed by
renormalization, its effects are not the additive terms in Equation~\ref{eq:retrieval-margin}.
The eight-document probe makes these interventions tractable, while the unchanged full-corpus
BEIR evaluation supplies the retrieval outcome.

\subsection{Update geometry is not an outcome}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise second-moment
preconditioner. Muon instead applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum
matrix. For the compact SVD $M=U\Sigma V^\top$, its idealized transform is $UV^\top$, which equalizes
nonzero singular values \citep{jordan2024muon}. NorMuon adds a row-wise historical rescaling after
orthogonalization \citep{li2025normuon}.

Repeated application of these rules changes both weights and the gradients of subsequent updates.
We study the accumulated result at saved checkpoints, rather than identify a trajectory with one
operator transform. Spectral equalization can emphasize weak directions
\citep{pang2026htmuon}, while optimizer switching can disturb pretrained features
\citep{qu2026finetune}. These motivate competing explanations to test through reached states,
not an assumption that a flatter spectrum must improve retrieval.

\section{Related Work}

Dense dual encoders rank a corpus through vector similarity. BEIR tests transfer across
retrieval tasks \citep{thakur2021beir}; DenseOn supplies the pretrained checkpoint and open
adaptation recipe \citep{sourty2026denseon}. HIL combines isotropic and anisotropic
representations in late-interaction retrieval \citep{kim2024hil}, motivating relevance-sensitive
evaluation rather than maximizing a spread statistic alone.

\citet{takeshita2025dimensions} study random and single-coordinate removal in text embeddings.
\citet{wu2026select} learn query-specific dimension selection from positive--negative
alignment. Relevance-sensitive dimension importance is therefore not itself our contribution.
We track finite deletion sensitivities along optimizer trajectories and test whether their
native-coordinate differences survive shared rotations and predict full-corpus retrieval.

Muon scales well in language-model pretraining, and NorMuon adds neuron-wise adaptation
\citep{liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. Associative-memory and imbalance studies connect spectral
updates to more balanced learning \citep{wang2025tailmemory,vasudeva2025spectral}; feature-transfer
benefits have also been reported \citep{ruan2026features}. These are functional explanations,
not just descriptions of an update rule. Yet spectral descent can lose useful simplicity
bias \citep{dragutinovic2026simplicity}, and optimizer switching can disrupt pretrained
knowledge \citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Spectral correction, input covariance,
and layer-dependent geometry offer further qualifications
\citep{pang2026htmuon,du2026newtonmuon,massena2026schatten}.

Basis and scale require separate care. Outlier-safe pretraining combines Muon with architectural
changes \citep{park2025outlier}; parameter-gauge equivariance is distinct from our post-encoding
rotations \citep{singh2026basis}. Hyperball connects weight and update norms to angular dynamics
\citep{wen2026hyperball}. Our initial fixed-probe calibration does not match entire trajectories.
We ask which reached-state measurements predict held-out-rate retrieval beyond the training
recipe, then separately cross fixed source states with reset continuation rules. Neither a
spectral fingerprint nor a basis-sensitive coordinate effect alone establishes a retrieval
mechanism.

\section{Tracing an Optimizer Through Weight Space}

\subsection{One controlled adaptation problem}

We adapt the released DenseOn unsupervised checkpoint on one deterministic view of
\NumTrainingQueries{} queries. Each group contains one positive and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct
sampled hard negatives. Queries are allocated proportionally across seven source datasets; a
candidate is eligible only when its mined score is below $0.95$ times the positive score, and the
seven negatives are sampled without replacement from the first ten eligible candidates using
stable seed-42 hashes. Every optimizer receives identical group identities and order. We use cosine
InfoNCE at temperature 0.02, no in-batch or cross-device negatives, maximum context length
\ContextLength{}, global batch size 128, one epoch, and bfloat16 forward passes.

The grid contains four learning rates per optimizer: $\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},
3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$ for AdamW and $\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$
for Muon and NorMuon. Muon-family runs route hidden two-dimensional matrices through the matrix
rule and auxiliary parameters through AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. Primary AdamW uses its
grid rate for all trainable parameters. These cells compare complete optimizer recipes, not only
the hidden-matrix update rule. Each run keeps checkpoints at
20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of 3,907 optimizer steps. Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same
\NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite.

We use eight NVIDIA L20Z GPUs as two disjoint four-GPU training pools, retaining four ranks per
run. Retrieval evaluation is task-parallel across otherwise idle devices and uses exact retrieval
rather than approximate ranking. AdamW uses fused CUDA AdamW; Muon and NorMuon use the documented
decomposed-bfloat16 Newton--Schulz implementation. All runs retain the same example identities,
ordering, checkpoint boundaries, and scoring semantics.

\subsection{Comparing rate surfaces and testing retrieval descriptors}

The primary estimand averages the four predeclared learning-rate cells within each optimizer at the
final checkpoint. This makes the optimizer comparison a statement about the tested rate surface,
not a post-hoc best cell. Task-level common resampling supplies simultaneous max-$T$ intervals for
the three pairwise contrasts. We also report all five optimizer-by-stage means and a secondary
validation-selected comparison, but neither can replace the primary surface-level result.

We test whether a state measurement describes retrieval beyond identifying which optimizer
produced it. Nine predeclared weight-space features and four representation-space features are
added separately to a baseline containing optimizer, checkpoint stage, and centered log learning
rate within optimizer. Four folds each hold out one ordered rate index across all optimizers and
stages. Predictive support requires lower pooled held-out RMSE and improvement in at least three
folds. The representation features are helpful mass share, normalized helpful participation,
degrading mass, and relative shortlist nDCG after removing 50\% of coordinates. We report every
candidate, including unsupported or unidentified comparisons. This tests generalization to an
unseen rate dose, not an unseen retrieval task or causal mediation.

\section{How the Optimizers Reshape the Retriever}

\subsection{Weight-state trajectories}

For each of the 88 hidden matrices $W_\ell$, we measure the saved-checkpoint segment
$W_{\ell,t}-W_{\ell,t-1}$ and cumulative displacement $W_{\ell,t}-W_{\ell,0}$. Magnitude is the
joint Frobenius norm of the displacement divided by the joint current weight norm, not an average
of matrix-wise ratios. Parameter-weighted summaries describe effective rank, row-norm coefficient
of variation, and top-1\% row-energy share. Rank-16 left/right subspace overlap compares all
unordered run pairs at each stage; optimizer-pair summaries average all learning-rate pairs
equally, without selecting a reference rate.

We retain the original approximate spectral measurements and separately report full-spectrum
sensitivity. Entropy rank computed from all singular values is a different quantity from entropy
within a renormalized truncated spectrum; the full-spectrum rank summaries explicitly weight
nonzero matrices. Neither measurement replaces the other. These are properties of the state
produced by many updates, not of a single optimizer step.

\subsection{Does Muon use embedding dimensions differently?}

Weight motion matters through the representation it creates. The frozen probe contains 16 queries
from each of 14 tasks, each with one relevant document and seven fixed competing documents.
We measure query/document covariance rank and recompute shortlist retrieval after removing
20 shared random coordinate sets at each of 10, 25, 50, and 75\% removal.

The primary attribution uses positive--hardest-negative margin. For task $t$ and coordinate $j$,
let $a_{tj}$ be the task-mean change in margin after deleting $j$ and renormalizing. We average
over queries before separating helpful and degrading effects:
$h_{tj}=\max(-a_{tj},0)$ and $b_{tj}=\max(a_{tj},0)$.
Writing $H_t=\sum_j h_{tj}$ and $B_t=\sum_j b_{tj}$, the three summaries are
\begin{equation}
\frac{H_t}{H_t+B_t},\qquad
\frac{H_t^2}{768\sum_j h_{tj}^2},\qquad B_t.
\label{eq:coordinate-utility}
\end{equation}
They measure helpful mass \emph{share}, normalized helpful participation, and degrading mass.
Zero denominators are assigned zero by the fixed definition. We compute each summary within task
before averaging tasks; analogous shortlist-nDCG attributions are descriptive. These deletion
sensitivities are not additive score contributions because renormalization and changes in the
hardest competing document couple their effects.

Final-stage optimizer summaries average all four rates. We use 50,000 common task-bootstrap
resamples and one fixed-standard-error max-$T$ family over the nine combinations of three features
and three optimizer contrasts. More constructive coordinate use requires all three simultaneous
95\% intervals in the desired directions: greater helpful share and participation, less degrading
mass. Failure of this joint criterion does not establish equivalence.

Coordinate attribution is basis dependent. We repeat the endpoint analysis after three shared Haar
rotations, each of which preserves all full-vector cosine scores. A native-coordinate difference can
support a claim about native-coordinate utilization. Directional agreement across the three tested
rotations is a robustness check, not proof of invariance under arbitrary bases or of greater
dimensional capacity.

\section{How Do Reached States Respond to Further Optimization?}

Retrieval at a fixed checkpoint depends on its weights, not on an optimizer applied only later.
Our follow-up therefore asks a conditional question: does the reached state change the response to
further optimization? We fix the 60\% AdamW $3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ and Muon $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$
checkpoints, reset optimizer history, and continue each with reset AdamW and reset Muon. The source
pair is fixed, not selected from the new BEIR outcomes or averaged over the primary rate surface.

Each cell's hidden learning rate is calibrated to a global hidden update-to-weight Frobenius ratio
of $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ on the fixed 32-example gradient-history probe, then held fixed as the
schedule's base rate. This does not match the first realized shuffled-batch update or subsequent
update norms. Both rules use the same hidden/auxiliary partition and auxiliary AdamW at
$3\!\times\!10^{-6}$; calibration excludes weight decay. The four cells share each of three fixed
data orders and a 50,000-query horizon.

For each seed--task pair, let $Y_{so}$ be final nDCG@10 after this continuation, with source
$s\in\{A,M\}$ and continuation rule $o\in\{A,M\}$. The frozen contrasts are
\begin{align}
\Delta_S &= \tfrac12[(Y_{MA}-Y_{AA})+(Y_{MM}-Y_{AM})],\\
\Delta_O &= \tfrac12[(Y_{AM}-Y_{AA})+(Y_{MM}-Y_{MA})],\\
\Delta_I &= (Y_{MM}-Y_{MA})-(Y_{AM}-Y_{AA}).
\end{align}
They concern post-continuation endpoints, not gains relative to each source checkpoint. In
particular, $Y_{MM}-Y_{AA}=\Delta_S+\Delta_O$; the interaction is not a third additive component.
A positive averaged contrast need not hold within both levels, and positive interaction can mean
less harm rather than improvement. Each contrast receives its own 95\% two-way seed/task bootstrap
interval from 100,000 resamples; these are marginal, not simultaneous intervals. An inconclusive
contrast is not equivalent to zero or dominated by one whose interval excludes zero.

\StateOperatorMechanismFinding

The direction of the two averaged main effects differs: the Muon-source state retains an
advantage, but reset Muon continuation does not. This is evidence about reached states under
further adaptation, not that an AdamW step explains the original Muon result. The source states
inherit the complete primary recipes, including their different auxiliary learning rates;
the common auxiliary rule in continuation does not remove that source-history difference.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The rate surface and the selected recipe answer different questions.}
The all-rate comparison estimates performance averaged over the specified grids; validation-based
selection estimates the outcome of choosing a recipe within those grids. Neither defines a
universal optimizer ranking. Five checkpoint stages show when retrieval differences emerge and
whether they persist. Conclusions remain specific to DenseOn and the tested rate ranges.

\paragraph{Weight-space separation and functional change need not coincide.}
Muon can produce spectrally distinct displacements without improving coordinate utility. Conversely,
small changes in weights can alter which query--document distinctions a representation preserves.
The held-out-dose tests ask which measurements track corpus ranking beyond optimizer identity,
stage, and dose. Some candidates improve prediction under the original comparator, but their
success is not stable across recipe-only alternatives. This distinction matters: a feature can
summarize omitted recipe variation without supplying a robust explanation of optimizer-dependent
retrieval. The present results leave that explanatory link unresolved; visual separation cannot
supply it.

\paragraph{Dimension count and dimension utility are different.}
Random-removal robustness can arise from redundancy, distributed coding, or cancellation between
helpful and degrading coordinates. Effective rank can also disagree with the functional ablation
readout. The coordinate analysis therefore asks which parts of the representation help retrieval,
how distributed that utility is, and whether any optimizer difference survives a change of basis.

\paragraph{Continuation tests state-dependent responses, not a gain decomposition.}
The positive averaged source-state contrast and negative reset-operator contrast demonstrate why
these are separate questions. The interaction does not resolve whether the continuation preference
changes between sources. Neither result isolates a measured geometric mediator or decomposes the
full training gain; that would require intervening on a feature rather than entire source states.

\section{Limitations}

Primary inference concerns one 149M-parameter DenseOn checkpoint, one English supervised corpus,
and one epoch of full-parameter adaptation. The base model was pretrained with an Adam-family
optimizer, so this is a study of optimizer switching rather than Muon-from-scratch pretraining.
Four learning rates provide a broad but finite grid. The all-rate contrast reduces best-cell
selection dependence but remains a one-seed, pinned-grid result; the secondary selected-recipe
comparison does not replace replication across training views. The selected AdamW rate is the
upper tested boundary, so the grid does not establish where its optimum lies.

Decontamination reduces known benchmark overlap but cannot establish the absence of every semantic
duplicate. Aggregate MTEB outputs lack per-query rankings, so uncertainty is computed over tasks and
we make no query-level BEIR significance claim. The geometry and dimension bridges are predictive
rather than causal and may miss useful properties outside the frozen candidates. Coordinate
ablation uses an eight-document probe, and coordinate identities are not invariant under rotation;
the rotation control limits, rather than eliminates, this dependence.

The crossed reset-state factorial concerns one fixed 60\% checkpoint pair and a 50K-query
continuation horizon. Its three order seeds do not replicate source-model training, and its marginal
intervals are not family-wise controlled. It changes entire source states and continuation rules,
not a measured geometry feature, and therefore does not identify mediation over the full 500K
trajectory or establish why a feature predicts retrieval.

\section{Ethical Considerations}

This study does not collect new human-subject data or deploy a retrieval system to users. Released
models and corpora can nevertheless contain social biases, harmful material, copyrighted text, or
personal information. Higher benchmark nDCG is not evidence of fairness, safety, privacy, or
deployment readiness. A production retriever requires corpus-specific auditing, filtering, access
controls, and human review appropriate to its users and failure costs.

We report useful wall time, throughput, GPU-hours, and optimizer-state size because an optimizer
recommendation has resource consequences. These measurements are specific to the pinned hardware
and software stack and do not justify unbounded hyperparameter search. Public artifacts must respect
the licenses and usage terms of upstream models, datasets, and evaluation collections.
