\begin{abstract}
Changing the optimizer changes the weight state that serves retrieval. We compare AdamW, Muon, and
NorMuon from one pretrained DenseOn checkpoint on 500,000 shared examples; five checkpoints from
12 configurations are evaluated on 14 decontaminated BEIR tasks. \CorrectedAbstractFinding{} We
trace hidden-weight displacement, subspace change, and functional use of 768 dimensions; candidate
mechanisms must predict retrieval at unseen learning-rate doses. Because Muon's update spectrum is
induced by its definition, we treat it as an operator fingerprint rather than an explanation. We
then test how two fixed reached states respond to reset continuation operators.
\StateOperatorAbstractFinding{}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Dense retrievers are usually adapted with AdamW. This convention hides a scientific choice:
starting from the same pretrained checkpoint, an optimizer selects a path through weight space and
therefore the representation used for corpus retrieval. Muon makes a qualitatively different
choice. It approximately orthogonalizes momentum updates for hidden matrices and remains trainable
at numerical learning rates far above the AdamW range
\citep{loshchilov2019decoupled,liu2025muon}. The visible consequence---a flatter update spectrum---
is largely built into the rule. Our question is not whether that signature appears, but what weight
state repeated Muon updates create and why that state retrieves differently.

This distinction matters because an optimizer update is not a retrieval outcome. Each accepted
step changes the weights, future gradients, and optimizer state on which later steps act. Two rules
can therefore have recognizable local geometry yet reach states with similar functions; conversely,
they can have similar immediate effects and diverge after a long closed-loop trajectory. A useful
mechanism must connect
\begin{equation*}
\text{updates}\rightarrow\text{weights}\rightarrow\text{embeddings}
\rightarrow\text{retrieval}.
\end{equation*}
We organize evidence around these links, keeping predictive associations separate from intervention
contrasts rather than equating the first link with the last.

We adapt DenseOn from one common initialization on a deterministic 500,000-query dataset with one
positive and seven hard negatives per query. AdamW, Muon, and NorMuon each receive four
predeclared learning rates and five checkpoint evaluations. Our primary comparison averages the
complete rate surface within optimizer over 14 decontaminated BEIR tasks; the full trajectories show
when differences emerge. We then measure hidden-weight displacement and subspace change, and ask a
more functional question inspired by coordinate ablation: does an optimizer change how broadly and
constructively the 768-dimensional embedding space contributes to retrieval?

Figure~\ref{fig:weight-space-map} summarizes the identification strategy. Descriptive geometry is
not promoted to a mechanism merely because it separates optimizers. A candidate must improve
retrieval prediction when an entire learning-rate dose is held out. The $2\times2$ continuation study crosses
two fixed reached checkpoints with reset AdamW or Muon, calibrating hidden update directions on a
common probe. It tests whether the relative continuation response depends on the reached weights,
not whether the original training gain can be partitioned into three mechanisms.

This investigation makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a complete optimizer--rate--time surface for DenseOn adaptation, evaluated by
    full-corpus retrieval rather than a single selected checkpoint;
    \item a trajectory analysis that connects hidden-weight motion to representation rank, random
    dimension removal, and helpful versus degrading coordinate contributions, with a shared-rotation
    control and out-of-dose prediction; and
    \item a crossed reset continuation that tests state-dependent responses to further optimization,
    without treating an operator fingerprint as a causal explanation of the original gain.
\end{itemize}

\section{From Optimizer Updates to Retrieval States}

\subsection{The loss sees a shortlist; evaluation sees a corpus}

For query $q_i$, positive $d_i^+$, and seven sampled negatives $C_i^-$, let
$z_{id}=s(q_i,d)/\tau$. The contrastive loss is
\begin{align}
\ell_i &= -z_i^+ + \log Z_i, \\
Z_i &= \exp(z_i^+) + \sum_{d\in C_i^-}\exp(z_{id}).
\end{align}
Full-corpus retrieval instead depends on the rank of $d_i^+$ among all documents in
$\mathcal D_i$. Holding out queries prevents direct query memorization, but it does not make
$C_i^-$ representative of $\mathcal D_i\setminus C_i^-$. Consequently, validation loss can improve
while nDCG falls. We call a change in optimizer ordering between these measurements the
\emph{shortlist--corpus gap}.

\subsection{Update geometry is not an outcome}

For a matrix parameter $W$ with gradient $G_t$, AdamW applies an element-wise second-moment
preconditioner. Muon instead applies a Newton--Schulz approximation to the polar factor of a momentum
matrix. If $M=U\Sigma V^\top$, its idealized transform is $UV^\top$, which approximately equalizes
nonzero singular values \citep{jordan2024muon}. NorMuon adds a row-wise historical rescaling after
orthogonalization \citep{li2025normuon}.

These are operator properties, not retrieval explanations. A flatter update can amplify weak or
noisy directions, and large weight motion can leave the ranking nearly unchanged
\citep{pang2026htmuon,qu2026finetune}. We therefore study saved weight states and their representations along complete trajectories.
Coordinate interventions and out-of-dose prediction test functional consequences of those states;
crossed continuation tests their response to further optimization. None of these is a direct
intervention on a measured weight-geometry feature, so predictive support is not causal mediation.

\section{Related Work}

Dense dual encoders compress queries and documents into single vectors and rank by similarity.
BEIR evaluates whether such representations transfer across heterogeneous retrieval tasks
\citep{thakur2021beir}. DenseOn provides the unsupervised, not-yet-fine-tuned checkpoint and open
training recipe used here \citep{sourty2026denseon}. Work on representation isotropy and effective
rank motivates possible bridges between optimization and retrieval, but those statistics are not
surrogates for ranking quality \citep{kim2024hil}.

Coordinate ablations provide a more functional view of dimension use. \citet{takeshita2025dimensions}
find that text encoders often retain most retrieval quality after random removal of half their
dimensions; their tested isotropy, correlation, and outlier measures do not account for the
observation, while deleting some individual coordinates improves performance. We use this distinction to ask whether an optimizer
merely changes representation rank or changes the distribution of helpful, redundant, and harmful
dimensions along its trajectory.

Muon has shown attractive scaling in language-model pretraining, while NorMuon augments its
matrix-level transform with neuron-wise adaptation \citep{liu2025muon,li2025normuon}. Concurrent
work warns that switching an Adam-pretrained model to Muon can disrupt pretrained knowledge as
update strength grows \citep{qu2026finetune,liu2026consistency}. Other analyses relate Muon to
feature rank or margins, identify risks from spectral equalization, or argue for covariance-aware
and layer-dependent matrix geometry \citep{ruan2026features,pang2026htmuon,du2026newtonmuon,
massena2026schatten}. Our question is downstream and retrieval-specific: which geometric changes
survive optimizer state feedback, preserve unseen scores, and predict corpus ranking?

\section{Tracing an Optimizer Through Weight Space}

\subsection{One controlled adaptation problem}

We adapt the released DenseOn unsupervised checkpoint on one deterministic view of
\NumTrainingQueries{} queries. Each group contains one positive and \NumHardNegatives{} distinct
sampled hard negatives. Queries are allocated proportionally across seven source datasets; a
candidate is eligible only when its mined score is below $0.95$ times the positive score, and the
seven negatives are sampled without replacement from the first ten eligible candidates using
stable seed-42 hashes. Every optimizer receives identical group identities and order. We use cosine
InfoNCE at temperature 0.02, no in-batch or cross-device negatives, maximum context length
\ContextLength{}, global batch size 128, one epoch, and bfloat16 forward passes.

The grid contains four learning rates per optimizer: $\{10^{-6},3\!\times\!10^{-6},10^{-5},
3\!\times\!10^{-5}\}$ for AdamW and $\{10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}$
for Muon and NorMuon. Muon-family runs route hidden two-dimensional matrices through the matrix
rule and auxiliary parameters through AdamW at $3\!\times\!10^{-6}$. Each run keeps checkpoints at
20, 40, 60, 80, and 100\% of 3,907 optimizer steps. Every checkpoint is evaluated on the same
\NumBEIRTasks-task decontaminated BEIR suite.

We use eight NVIDIA L20Z GPUs as two disjoint four-GPU training pools, retaining four ranks per
run. Retrieval evaluation is task-parallel across otherwise idle devices and uses exact retrieval
rather than approximate ranking. AdamW uses fused CUDA AdamW; Muon and NorMuon use the documented
decomposed-bfloat16 Newton--Schulz implementation. All runs retain the same example identities,
ordering, checkpoint boundaries, and scoring semantics.

\subsection{Evidence and inference}

The primary estimand averages the four predeclared learning-rate cells within each optimizer at the
final checkpoint. This makes the optimizer comparison a statement about the tested rate surface,
not a post-hoc best cell. Task-level common resampling supplies simultaneous max-$T$ intervals for
the three pairwise contrasts. We also report all five optimizer-by-stage means and a secondary
validation-selected comparison, but neither can replace the primary surface-level result.

Mechanism evidence is deliberately harder to obtain than optimizer separation. Nine frozen
weight-space features are added one at a time to a baseline containing optimizer, stage, and
within-optimizer learning-rate dose. We evaluate each feature by leaving one dose index out across
all optimizers. A feature is supported only if it lowers pooled held-out RMSE and improves at least
three of four folds. Coordinate-level representation features follow the same out-of-dose rule.
Descriptive correlation, optimizer classification, and visually separated trajectories cannot pass
this gate.

\section{How the Optimizers Reshape the Retriever}

\subsection{Weight-state trajectories}

For every hidden matrix $W_\ell$, we measure the saved-checkpoint segment
$W_{\ell,t}-W_{\ell,t-1}$ and cumulative displacement $W_{\ell,t}-W_{\ell,0}$ relative to the
current weight norm. Randomized sketches estimate stable and entropy effective rank, and rank-16
left/right subspace overlap compares all unordered run pairs at each stage. Optimizer-pair summaries
average all learning-rate pairs equally, without selecting a reference rate. These
are measurements of the state produced by many updates; we do not relabel a retained-checkpoint
segment as a single optimizer step.

\subsection{Does Muon use embedding dimensions differently?}

Weight motion matters only through the representation it creates. On a frozen 224-query probe, we
therefore measure query/document covariance rank and recompute retrieval after removing shared
random coordinate sets at 10, 25, 50, and 75\%. For coordinate $j$, let
$a_j=m(Z_{-j})-m(Z)$, where $m$ is shortlist nDCG or positive--hardest-negative margin after
renormalization. Positive $a_j$ marks a degrading coordinate; negative $a_j$ marks a helpful one.
We summarize helpful mass, degrading mass, and the participation ratio of helpful attribution rather
than treating effective rank alone as dimension use. These leave-one-coordinate-out sensitivities
are not an additive decomposition of the score: renormalization and changes in document ranking
couple their effects.

Coordinate attribution is basis dependent. We repeat the endpoint analysis after three shared Haar
rotations, each of which preserves all full-vector cosine scores. A native-coordinate difference can
support a claim about native-coordinate utilization. Directional agreement across the three tested
rotations is a robustness check, not proof of invariance under arbitrary bases or of greater
dimensional capacity.

\section{How Do Reached States Respond to Further Optimization?}

Retrieval at a fixed checkpoint depends on its weights, not on an optimizer applied only later.
Our follow-up therefore asks a conditional question: does the reached state change the response to
further optimization? We fix the 60\% AdamW $3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ and Muon $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$
checkpoints, reset optimizer history, and continue each with reset AdamW and reset Muon. The source
pair is fixed, not selected from the new BEIR outcomes or averaged over the primary rate surface.

Each cell's hidden learning rate is calibrated to a global hidden update-to-weight Frobenius ratio
of $5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ on the fixed 32-example gradient-history probe, then held fixed as the
schedule's base rate. This does not match the first realized shuffled-batch update or subsequent
update norms. Both rules use the same hidden/auxiliary partition and auxiliary AdamW at
$3\!\times\!10^{-6}$; calibration excludes weight decay. The four cells share each of three fixed
data orders and a 50,000-query horizon.

For each seed--task pair, let $Y_{so}$ be final nDCG@10 after this continuation, with source
$s\in\{A,M\}$ and continuation rule $o\in\{A,M\}$. The frozen contrasts are
\begin{align}
\Delta_S &= \tfrac12[(Y_{MA}-Y_{AA})+(Y_{MM}-Y_{AM})],\\
\Delta_O &= \tfrac12[(Y_{AM}-Y_{AA})+(Y_{MM}-Y_{MA})],\\
\Delta_I &= (Y_{MM}-Y_{MA})-(Y_{AM}-Y_{AA}).
\end{align}
They concern post-continuation endpoints, not gains relative to each source checkpoint. In
particular, $Y_{MM}-Y_{AA}=\Delta_S+\Delta_O$; the interaction is not a third additive component.
A positive averaged contrast need not hold within both levels, and positive interaction can mean
less harm rather than improvement. Each contrast receives its own 95\% two-way seed/task bootstrap
interval from 100,000 resamples; these are marginal, not simultaneous intervals. An inconclusive
contrast is not equivalent to zero or dominated by one whose interval excludes zero.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Optimizer quality is a property of a trajectory surface.}
The all-rate estimand prevents the argument from collapsing onto one fortunate learning rate, while
five checkpoint stages reveal whether an advantage emerges early, accumulates gradually, or
disappears. The result remains specific to the tested DenseOn adaptation problem; it is stronger
than a best-cell comparison and weaker than a universal optimizer ranking.

\paragraph{Geometry must earn explanatory status.}
Muon's spectrum and NorMuon's row adaptation describe how their updates are constructed. Our
tests require more: geometry must predict corpus ranking at unseen doses beyond optimizer identity,
stage, and dose. If no feature passes, the optimizer effect remains unexplained; the most visually
distinctive statistic does not become a mechanism by default.

\paragraph{Dimension count and dimension utility are different.}
Random-removal robustness can arise from redundancy, distributed coding, or cancellation between
helpful and degrading coordinates. Effective rank can also disagree with the functional ablation
readout. The coordinate analysis therefore asks which parts of the representation help retrieval,
how distributed that utility is, and whether any optimizer difference survives a change of basis.

\paragraph{Continuation tests state-dependent responses, not a gain decomposition.}
The crossed design asks whether a source-state advantage persists on average after further
adaptation and whether the relative continuation preference changes between the fixed states.
Neither an averaged main effect nor an interaction alone establishes benefit within both states,
additivity, or full-trajectory co-adaptation. Explaining a particular geometric feature causally
would require an intervention on that feature, beyond the present state-level comparison.

The broader lesson is methodological: a retrieval optimizer should be explained by the functional
weight states its closed-loop trajectory creates. Intrinsic properties of one update can motivate a
hypothesis; state measurements tied to unseen retrieval and conditional continuation responses
provide complementary evidence without constituting a causal decomposition of an empirical gain.

\section{Limitations}

Primary inference concerns one 149M-parameter DenseOn checkpoint, one English supervised corpus,
and one epoch of full-parameter adaptation. The base model was pretrained with an Adam-family
optimizer, so this is a study of optimizer switching rather than Muon-from-scratch pretraining.
Four learning rates provide a broad but finite grid. The all-rate contrast reduces best-cell
selection dependence but remains a one-seed, pinned-grid result; the secondary selected-recipe
comparison does not replace replication across training views.

Decontamination reduces known benchmark overlap but cannot establish the absence of every semantic
duplicate. Aggregate MTEB outputs lack per-query rankings, so uncertainty is computed over tasks and
we make no query-level BEIR significance claim. The geometry and dimension bridges are predictive
rather than causal and may miss useful properties outside the frozen candidates. Coordinate
ablation uses an eight-document probe, and coordinate identities are not invariant under rotation;
the rotation control limits, rather than eliminates, this dependence.

The crossed reset-state factorial concerns one fixed 60\% checkpoint pair and a 50K-query
continuation horizon. Its three order seeds do not replicate source-model training, and its marginal
intervals are not family-wise controlled. It changes entire source states and continuation rules,
not a measured geometry feature, and therefore does not identify mediation over the full 500K
trajectory or establish why a feature predicts retrieval.

\section{Ethical Considerations}

This study does not collect new human-subject data or deploy a retrieval system to users. Released
models and corpora can nevertheless contain social biases, harmful material, copyrighted text, or
personal information. Higher benchmark nDCG is not evidence of fairness, safety, privacy, or
deployment readiness. A production retriever requires corpus-specific auditing, filtering, access
controls, and human review appropriate to its users and failure costs.

We report useful wall time, throughput, GPU-hours, and optimizer-state size because an optimizer
recommendation has resource consequences. These measurements are specific to the pinned hardware
and software stack and do not justify unbounded hyperparameter search. Public artifacts must respect
the licenses and usage terms of upstream models, datasets, and evaluation collections.
